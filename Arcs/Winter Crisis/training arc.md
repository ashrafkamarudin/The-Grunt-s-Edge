The Grunt's Edge: Training Arc
Purpose of the arc
The training arc should span three to four chapters, with four
chapters currently the preferred structure. It should show Eric learning
the basics of military life and combat while also telling a smaller
story through the recruits around him.

The arc should not read like a sequence of training exercises. It needs
character interactions, friction, small personal stories, and a growing
sense that something is going wrong beyond the training station.

The arc ends when training is cut short and the recruits are sent toward
the frontier earlier than expected.
Core training beats
The recruits should receive introductory training in:
1. Spears, shields, and formation
   - Basic spear handling and shield use.
   - Maintaining formation and following commands.
   - Learning to rely on the people beside them rather than fighting
     as individuals.
   - Physical conditioning and the endurance needed to march and
     fight in equipment.
2. Monster awareness and preparation
   - Recruits learn about the monsters they may actually be expected
     to face.
   - Keep the training roster limited to one or two common monster
     types suited to the approaching winter.
   - A possible third creature may be introduced as especially
     dangerous. The instruction for this creature should be clear:
     avoid it and retreat rather than attempt to fight it.
   - Teach practical recognition: appearance, behaviour, signs,
     weaknesses, and basic group responses.
   - This is introductory knowledge. Recruits can learn more detailed
     information from their eventual units and experienced soldiers.
3. Magic aptitude assessment
   - The army tests recruits because people with magical aptitude may
     be valuable and worth directing into further training.
   - The assessment introduces basic concepts such as mana and the
     ability to sense or gather it.
   - Eric appears to have mediocre aptitude. He can produce some
     response, but not enough to attract further investment from the
     army.
   - Eric still gains a basic understanding of what magic is and what
     the assessment is looking for.
   - The test should not reveal or explain Eric's hidden spatial
     anomaly. His result is simply judged mediocre at this stage.
   - A recruit with genuine magical aptitude is identified and
     selected for further assessment or specialised magical training.
4. Training interrupted
   - Monster activity increases earlier than expected and becomes
     more frequent as the arc progresses.
   - Reports and incidents gradually suggest that the frontier and
     nearby settlements are under growing pressure.
   - The training station receives orders to send personnel out
     before the recruits have completed their full training.
   - The recruits are rushed toward the frontier. They are not
     suddenly experienced soldiers; they leave with incomplete
     preparation.
The bullying subplot
A secondary story runs alongside the training.
The bullied recruit
- A genuinely weak recruit struggles with conditioning and basic
  training.
- He has difficulty keeping formation and makes mistakes during
  exercises.
- Some stronger recruits begin by teasing him, but their behaviour
  gradually crosses into bullying.
- They may mock his performance, push him around when instructors are
  absent, blame him for group failures, or dump unwanted duties on
  him.
- He tries to endure the treatment and does not want to make himself a
  bigger target.
- He does not need to turn out to be secretly powerful or
  exceptionally talented. His weakness can remain genuine.
Darrin: the hero-like recruit
- The recruit who eventually stands up to the bullies is Darrin.
- He has a strong sense of right and wrong and refuses to accept the
  mistreatment of someone weaker.
- He intervenes openly when the bullying crosses a line.
- His courage and decision to intervene come from his character, not
  from magical talent.
- He should not use magic to threaten or overpower the bullies.
- He may be idealistic and somewhat righteous, or have a more
  confrontational streak. His exact temperament and background remain
  to be developed.
- The intervention creates friction with the bullies. Darrin has no
  special authority over them, but he is willing to challenge them.
Eric's role
- Eric is a bystander to the incident rather than the person who
  rescues the bullied recruit.
- He notices what is happening and feels uncomfortable, but he does
  not automatically step into the centre of the conflict.
- He is still physically inexperienced, unfamiliar with the army's
  social dynamics, and unsure whether intervening would make things
  worse.
- He watches Darrin step forward and may wonder whether he would have
  done the same.
- Eric's reaction can be conveyed through observation and internal
  reflection without forcing him to explain every thought aloud.
Other recruits
- Darrin may react more emotionally or be tempted to get involved.
- Tomas may quietly notice details others miss.
- Garran can offer a more practical, experienced perspective, without
  becoming the central figure in the subplot.
- The bullies should feel like recruits behaving badly under pressure,
  not cartoon villains.
Darrin's magical aptitude
Darrin is eventually identified as having genuine magical aptitude
during the army's assessment.
- He is tested after the bullying incident has established his
  character.
- The examiner notices a notably strong response and may repeat the
  test or ask a more experienced examiner to confirm it.
- Darrin is selected for further assessment or specialised magical
  training.
- This is recognition of potential, not instant mastery. He still has
  to learn control, discipline, and the fundamentals of magic.
- He is not brave because he has magical talent. He stood up for the
  bullied recruit before anyone knew about his aptitude.
This creates a contrast between Eric and Darrin:
- Eric: His aptitude appears mediocre, so the army does not
  consider him worth investing in as a prospective mage.
- Darrin: His aptitude is strong enough to attract the army's
  attention and open a different path for him.
Eric may feel a little disappointment at his own result, but he should
not become bitter or jealous. He can recognise that Darrin has earned
the opportunity.

Their paths may diverge after training. They can be acquaintances who
shared an important experience rather than instant best friends. A later
reunion could carry weight because of that shared history.
Proposed chapter structure
This is a working structure, not a locked outline. The user may add or
change beats before the arc is finalised.
Chapter 11: Learning to Stand in Formation
- Begin the recruits' basic conditioning and physical training.
- Introduce spear and shield fundamentals.
- Teach simple commands and the importance of maintaining formation.
- Show Eric struggling physically. Understanding instructions does not
  make his body capable of carrying them out easily.
- Establish the weaker recruit and the first signs of teasing or
  bullying.
- Let Eric observe the social dynamics without making him the centre
  of the incident.
- Monster activity can begin appearing in background reports or
  passing comments.
Eric's progression: He begins learning how to function as part of a
group, but remains physically inexperienced.
Chapter 12: Fighting Together
- Continue spear-and-shield practice, with greater emphasis on
  formation and group responses.
- Introduce the one or two common winter-suitable monsters that
  recruits are expected to recognise and face.
- Teach practical signs, weaknesses, and basic responses rather than
  an encyclopaedic account of monster lore.
- Escalate the bullying of the weaker recruit.
- Darrin intervenes when the behaviour crosses a line, creating
  tension with the bullies.
- Eric watches the incident unfold.
- Reports of monster sightings or attacks become more concerning.
Eric's progression: He begins to understand that surviving a monster
encounter depends on discipline and cooperation, not individual heroics.
Chapter 13: Aptitude
- Conduct the army's magic aptitude assessment.
- Introduce basic concepts of mana and the simple exercise used to
  test a recruit's response.
- Eric's result appears mediocre. He learns the basics but is not
  selected for further magical investment.
- Darrin demonstrates strong aptitude. His result is checked or
  confirmed, and he is selected for further assessment or specialised
  training.
- Eric may feel a moment of disappointment, but does not resent
  Darrin.
- The bullying conflict may have lingering consequences, but should
  not overwhelm the chapter.
- Monster reports continue to worsen.
Eric's progression: He gains a basic understanding of magic, while
learning that the army sees no obvious magical potential in him.

Darrin's progression: His path begins to diverge from the ordinary
recruits as the army recognises his aptitude.
Chapter 14: Before They Are Ready
- Monster activity increases enough that the training station can no
  longer follow its original schedule.
- Patrols encounter monsters closer to roads, farms, or settlements.
- Reports and urgent orders reveal that the situation is deteriorating
  earlier than expected.
- Experienced soldiers are sent out, and the station is ordered to
  provide additional manpower.
- The recruits' training is cut short.
- Earlier rivalries and the bullying subplot are put into perspective
  as the recruits face the possibility of real danger.
- The arc ends with the recruits being deployed or beginning their
  movement toward the frontier. Their first actual encounter can
  belong to the following arc.
Eric's progression: He has learned the basics, but he knows he is
not ready for real combat.
Character and tone principles
- The training arc belongs to Eric, even when supporting characters
  have their own important moments.
- Eric should not become an exceptional fighter in a few weeks. His
  progress is modest and believable.
- Darrin, Tomas, and Garran should retain their established
  personalities:
  - Darrin: Broad-shouldered, confident, talkative, jokes and
    fills silence; can become quieter when tired.
  - Tomas: Narrower, quiet and nervous at first; rarely starts
    conversations, but can be perceptive.
  - Garran: Older recalled soldier with practical experience.
    Helpful, but not automatically Eric's personal instructor.
- The bullied recruit should be a real weakling, not a hidden prodigy.
- Darrin's moral courage should exist independently of his magical
  aptitude.
- Magic aptitude identifies potential, not immediate skill.
- Monster knowledge should remain practical and limited to what
  recruits need for their expected duties.
- The increasing monster threat should be seeded gradually throughout
  the arc, so the rushed deployment feels earned rather than sudden.
- Show selected meaningful training moments and the passage of time.
  Do not narrate every exercise or every day.
- Keep the recruits' knowledge of the wider military and kingdom
  limited to what they could reasonably know.

- Set Darrin's personality more precisely: idealistic protector,

  creature, if one is included.
- Define the exact magic aptitude test and what counts as a mediocre

- Decide how much time passes during the training arc.
- Decide whether Darrin leaves the ordinary recruit group immediately
  after the assessment or remains with them until the deployment
  order.
- Finalise the exact frontier deployment circumstances and whether the
  arc ends with the order, departure, or arrival.
