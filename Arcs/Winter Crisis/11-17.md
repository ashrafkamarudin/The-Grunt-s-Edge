Winter Fortress Arc and Mage Unit Setup
Working story reference | Chapters 11--17
This document consolidates the current planning decisions for the
Winter Fortress Arc and the transition into the next arc. Treat
details marked provisional as flexible. Do not silently turn
suggestions into canon.

1. Arc overview
Working arc title: Winter Fortress Arc
Chapter range: 11--17
Next arc begins: Chapter 18

The Winter Fortress Arc follows Eric and Derrin as they enter military
life at a frontier fortress during winter. They undergo physical
conditioning and military training, encounter the values and pressures
of the army, and learn that monster activity is increasing across the
wider region.

The arc also establishes a divergence in their paths: - Derrin
demonstrates courage and is found to have an average magical core,
leading to specialized mage training. - Eric is found to have no magical
core. He is disappointed to a small degree, but not devastated. His
development in this arc is physical rather than magical. - Eric's
endurance and strength make him useful to an operational mage unit as an
auxiliary porter.

The arc is primarily a setup and transition arc. It establishes the
fortress, the military situation, Eric's physical development, Derrin's
new direction, and the circumstances that send Eric into the field with
a mage unit.
What this arc is not
- It is not the arc where Eric learns to cast magic.
- It is not the arc where Eric becomes a capable fighter.
- It is not a single, massive monster siege on the fortress.
- It should not turn Eric into a prodigy or make him the best recruit
  at everything.
- It should not resolve his lack of a magical core through a mistaken
  test or an easy hidden-core reveal.
2. Core character development
Eric
Eric's main development during Chapters 11--17 is physical
conditioning.

He improves gradually through: - Running and endurance exercises. -
Strength and bodyweight training. - Marching and carrying loads. -
Cold-weather conditioning. - General military discipline and routine.

By the end of the arc: - He has noticeably better stamina and physical
strength. - Endurance is one of his strongest qualities. - He is among
the better-performing commoner recruits physically, but he is not the
best recruit overall. - He remains inexperienced in actual combat. - He
cannot use magic and has no magical core.

His physical progress should feel earned through repeated training,
effort, fatigue, recovery, and adaptation. Avoid sudden leaps in ability
or excessive praise from instructors.

Why the army selects him: His strength and endurance make him
suitable to carry supplies and keep pace with a mage unit on long
expeditions. He is not selected because of combat talent, magical
aptitude, special destiny, or secret potential.
Eric's reaction to the magical compatibility test
When Eric hears that the army will conduct magical compatibility tests,
his thoughts should come quickly and overlap: - Magic exists in this
world? - Could magic somehow help him return to Earth? - Does he
actually want to return? - Does he have any magical aptitude?

These are internal thoughts. He does not need to say them aloud.

The test shows that Eric has no magical core. This is a genuine
result. Do not make the test faulty simply to preserve a later magical
awakening.

Afterward, Eric finds somewhere quiet and sits alone to process it. His
reaction is restrained: - He is a little disappointed, but not
devastated. - He reminds himself that he never had magic, or even knew
magic, before arriving in this world. - He wonders why the result
disappoints him. Perhaps he had briefly allowed himself to imagine what
having magic might mean. - He gradually turns his attention from the
result to curiosity about magic itself.

He may wonder whether mana is: - A tangible but invisible substance,
perhaps something like air. - A form of energy, perhaps comparable to
electricity. - Produced inside a person, drawn from the environment, or
supplied in different ways depending on the spell. - The underlying
resource that magic manipulates, while a spell is the method or process
of manipulation.

These are questions, not conclusions. Eric does not solve the magic
system in this scene. He puts the questions aside and returns to
training.
Derrin
Derrin's important character beat is an act of courage during training.

A recruit who is weaker, less experienced, or otherwise vulnerable is
bullied or mistreated by stronger recruits. Derrin intervenes, despite
the possibility that doing so may inconvenience him or put him at risk.

The scene should show: - Derrin's willingness to stand up for someone. -
His sense of fairness and courage. - Eric observing and being
impressed. - Eric privately wondering whether he would have done the
same.

Derrin does not need to win a dramatic fight. The choice to intervene is
the point.

Derrin later tests as having an average magical core and is offered
specialized mage training. This creates a divergence between the two
friends, but it should not become an unnecessary melodramatic
separation: - They can remain friends. - They can still interact at the
fortress. - Derrin does not need to refuse his opportunity out of
guilt. - Eric does not need to resent him.

Their paths simply begin to differ. Derrin starts formal magical
development, while Eric continues physical development and later joins a
field unit.
3. The seven-chapter outline
The following is the current working structure. Chapter titles and
scene-level details can still change during drafting.
Chapter 11 --- A New Beginning
Eric and Derrin begin physical conditioning and military training at the
Winter Fortress.

Possible material: - Introduction to the training grounds, instructors,
recruits, and daily routine. - Running, endurance exercises, strength
work, marching, and carrying loads. - The cold-weather demands of
training. - The expectations and discipline of military life. - Early
indications that Eric has good endurance and responds well to physical
conditioning.

Eric should perform well, but not dominate every exercise. Establish the
baseline from which his later improvement can be measured.
Chapter 12 --- A Small Act of Courage
Training continues and the recruit social hierarchy becomes clearer.

A stronger group mistreats or bullies a weaker or less experienced
recruit. Derrin steps in.

Focus on: - The social dynamics among recruits. - Derrin's decision to
intervene. - Eric's observation and reaction. - A small but meaningful
moment that reveals character.

Avoid turning this into an unnecessarily large brawl or a showcase of
combat ability.
Chapter 13 --- The Compatibility Test
The army conducts magical compatibility testing for recruits who have
not previously been assessed.

The test should be introduced naturally, as a practical military
process. The army wants to identify recruits who may be suitable for
specialized magical training or service.

Key outcomes: - Derrin has an average magical core and is identified as
suitable for mage training. - Eric has no magical core.

Before his test, Eric's thoughts about magic and the possibility of
returning to Earth briefly surface. Keep this internal and somewhat
unsettled.
Chapter 14 --- Different Paths
Eric processes the result of his test.

He finds a quiet place to sit and think. He is slightly disappointed,
but does not collapse into despair. He questions why he had hoped for
something he had never possessed before.

His thoughts turn toward the nature of mana and magic: - Is mana a
substance or a kind of energy? - Does it exist in the environment? -
Does a person produce it internally? - How does a spell make use of it?

He has no answers yet. This is the beginning of his curiosity, not the
beginning of his ability to cast spells.

Derrin begins transitioning toward specialized mage training. The two
friends can remain in contact, even as their routines begin to diverge.

Eric returns to physical training and continues improving.
Chapter 15 --- A Growing Threat
Reports from patrols and nearby settlements indicate that monster
sightings are increasing across the region.

This should be a developing regional problem rather than one enormous,
coordinated assault on the fortress.

The army is stretched thin because incidents occur across a broad
area: - Patrols need to cover more ground. - Experienced mages and
knights are assigned to different locations. - Some units are redirected
to threatened settlements or routes. - The fortress has to manage its
available personnel carefully.

The wider situation moves from isolated incidents toward scattered,
recurring incursions. This creates pressure on the army without
requiring a full siege.
Chapter 16 --- Orders
The normal training program is interrupted or suspended because of the
worsening situation.

Recruits are reassigned according to practical needs.

Eric is selected as an auxiliary porter for an operational mage unit.
His selection is based on: - Physical conditioning. - Strength and
endurance. - Ability to march for long distances. - Ability to carry a
substantial load while keeping pace.

He is not being sent because he is a fighter or because someone believes
he has hidden magical potential. The army needs reliable support
personnel, and Eric is suitable for the role.
Chapter 17 --- The Mage Unit
Eric meets the mage unit and learns what his assignment entails.

The unit is an operational attack and monster-hunting team. It travels
to investigate reports, hunt monsters, clear dangerous areas, and
support threatened locations.

Eric receives practical instructions about: - The supplies he is
responsible for. - How loads are packed and distributed. - How the unit
travels and camps. - What he should do during an encounter. - The limits
of his role and the importance of staying out of the way during combat.

The chapter ends with preparations for deployment or the unit setting
out. This closes the Winter Fortress Arc and opens the next stage of
Eric's story.
4. The Winter Fortress and the military situation
The fortress is a frontier military installation responsible for
protecting a broad surrounding region.

It should feel like a functioning military post rather than merely a
training school: - It has patrols and operational units. - It receives
reports from settlements and outposts. - It dispatches personnel to
investigate threats. - It must allocate limited manpower across a large
area. - Training and routine duties can be disrupted by operational
demands.

The winter environment should influence daily life and operations: -
Cold-weather clothing and equipment. - Snow, ice, and difficult
terrain. - Greater effort required for travel and carrying supplies. -
Camps and shelters requiring preparation. - The practical risks of
exposure and weather during expeditions.

Monster activity is increasing at multiple locations. The army is
stretched because threats are spread across the region, not because one
monster army is attacking the fortress all at once.
5. Provisional winter monster roster
Keep the roster limited. One or two common monster types and one
exceptionally dangerous type are enough for this part of the story.

The following names and concepts are provisional:
Frostfang Wolves --- common threat
- Pack predators suited to cold environments.
- Fast and dangerous in groups.
- A plausible threat for patrols, travellers, and isolated
  settlements.
- Their pack behaviour can make them more dangerous than an individual
  wolf-like monster.
Snow Trolls --- dangerous threat
- Large, powerful, and durable.
- More dangerous than ordinary recruits should be expected to handle.
- Encounters should require experienced personnel, planning, or a
  tactical advantage.
Winter Wraiths --- extreme threat
- A rare or exceptionally dangerous winter-associated monster.
- An encounter should be treated as a serious emergency.
- Inexperienced recruits should retreat rather than attempt to fight
  one.
- Early references can be through reports, warnings, tracks,
  aftermath, or experienced soldiers' accounts. A direct encounter is
  not required in the Winter Fortress Arc.
Do not introduce all three simply to fill a checklist. Use only what the
chapter needs, and preserve the sense that some threats are beyond the
recruits' ability.
6. The mage unit
Mission and function
The mage unit is an attack and monster-hunting unit, not merely a
defensive garrison.

It may be sent to: - Investigate monster sightings. - Hunt or eliminate
dangerous monsters. - Search for and clear monster nests or lairs. -
Reinforce threatened areas. - Support other military units. - Travel
through difficult terrain to deal with threats before they grow worse.

The unit operates away from the fortress and must be able to sustain
itself during expeditions.
Eric's role: auxiliary porter
Eric joins as a porter and support member, not as a combat mage or
frontline soldier.

He carries essential equipment and supplies, potentially including: -
Food and rations. - Preserved meat. - Water containers. - First-aid
supplies, bandages, and medicines. - Camping gear, tents, blankets, and
sleeping equipment. - Cooking equipment. - Tools, ropes, and repair
materials. - Spare clothing and other emergency supplies. - Torches or
other practical equipment, depending on the world's technology.

The exact loadout should be decided when planning the expedition scenes.

The porter role is operationally important. If the unit lacks food,
medicine, or essential equipment, the mission can fail even if its mages
are powerful.

Eric's responsibility is to support the people who fight. At this stage,
he is not expected to fight monsters himself.
Why Eric is chosen
The assignment is a practical manpower decision: - He has developed good
physical conditioning. - He has above-average endurance. - He is strong
enough to carry supplies. - He can keep pace during long marches. - The
unit needs someone to carry equipment so that its combat personnel can
preserve their strength and mana for the mission.

Do not frame this as a prestigious selection or a sign that the army
recognizes some hidden destiny in him.
Suggested unit size and composition
A small unit of approximately five to seven people, including Eric,
should be manageable for the reader.

Possible roles: - Mage commander. - Senior or experienced mage. -
Offensive mage. - Defensive or support mage. - Possibly another
auxiliary soldier or specialist. - Eric as porter.

This is a flexible starting point, not a fixed roster. Introduce members
gradually rather than presenting the entire team in one dense exposition
scene.
7. The mage commander
The commander should be a mage from a commoner background, not a noble
or celebrated prodigy.

Current character direction: - Average or below-average magical
aptitude/power. - Considerable practical experience. - Earned command
through military service, reliability, and sound judgement. - Competent
at organizing a team and managing missions. - Understands mana
expenditure and the limits of ordinary mages. - Knows when to engage,
reposition, or retreat. - Understands the practical needs and
limitations of non-mage soldiers.

His authority comes from experience and responsibility, not a grand
reputation.
Personality and relationship with Eric
- Somewhat unassuming and practical.
- May have a dry sense of humour.
- Does not immediately become Eric's father figure.
- Does not need to take Eric on as a formal student.
- Can become Eric's most accessible window into observing real magic
  in action.
Eric may ask him questions after watching him cast. The commander can
answer when convenient, but he does not need to deliver lectures or
understand why Eric is interested in basic theoretical questions.

The commander should demonstrate magic as part of his actual work. Eric
learns by watching, asking, comparing what he sees with what he thinks
he understands, and gradually correcting his assumptions.
8. The next arc: the beginning of Eric's magic journey
The next arc begins after Chapter 17, when Eric is with the mage unit.

This is where two development tracks begin to run alongside one another.
Track A: physical combat training
Eric begins learning physical combat with the rest of the team or
through the unit's training routine.

This is the appropriate stage for: - Basic weapon handling. - Footwork
and defensive movement. - Balance and positioning. - Basic
self-protection. - Field awareness and survival habits. - Learning how
to avoid becoming a liability during an encounter.

He is still a beginner. His physical conditioning gives him a useful
foundation, but it does not make him an experienced fighter.

Important continuity distinction: These combat skills belong to the
next arc, not the Winter Fortress Arc. Chapters 11--17 focus on fitness
and conditioning.
Track B: understanding magic through observation
Eric's magic journey begins intellectually, not through casting.

He watches the commander and other mages use magic. He tries to
understand what he is seeing and asks questions when appropriate.

Possible questions include: - Is mana generated inside the body or drawn
from the surroundings? - Can a spell use both internal and environmental
mana? - Does mana gather around the caster, the target, or the space
between them? - What does concentration actually do? - What determines
the shape or effect of a spell? - How does a mage control expenditure? -
What happens when a mage runs low on mana?

These questions are exploratory. Do not assume Eric immediately
understands the answers.
Three distinct milestones
Keep these stages separate, with meaningful time and development between
them:
1. Conceptual understanding: Eric learns how the world describes
   mana, spells, casting, and magical limitations. He can understand
   ideas without being able to perform them.
2. Interacting with or sensing mana: At some later point, he may
   begin to perceive or interact with mana. This is a separate
   breakthrough, not an automatic consequence of learning theory.
3. Wielding magic: Producing a magical effect or casting a spell is
   another distinct milestone, likely much later.
The next arc should focus mainly on the first stage.
Eric's Earth-based reasoning
Eric can use ideas from his previous world as analogies: - Energy and
electricity. - Physical systems and cause-and-effect. - Inputs,
processes, and outputs. - The difference between a resource and the
method used to manipulate it.

His scientific intuition should give him useful questions, not instant
answers. He may misunderstand what he observes, make incorrect
assumptions, or use analogies that only partly fit.

Let the reader discover the system alongside him. Avoid having Eric
solve the entire magic system through one clever observation.
The no-core limitation
Eric's test result remains valid: he has no magical core.

Do not casually undo this through: - A faulty test. - A secret,
unusually powerful core. - A sudden revelation that he was special all
along.

If Eric eventually finds a way to interact with mana despite this
limitation, the explanation must come from a meaningful discovery of how
the world's magical system works. It should be earned, internally
consistent, and developed over time.
9. Mana and magic continuity notes
These are high-level reminders for this arc. The detailed rules of mana,
techniques, tiers, and spell limitations should remain governed by the
separate magic-system reference documents.
- Do not conflate mana, spells, and techniques. They may be related,
  but they are not automatically the same thing.
- Eric's questions about mana are not canon answers.
- A mage's ability to cast repeatedly or sustain a spell depends on
  their tier and the established limits of the magic system.
- Higher-tier individuals may sustain magic longer or perform larger
  effects, but do not invent exact capacities in this arc unless those
  rules have already been established in the main magic-system notes.
- The commander should demonstrate practical mana management through
  action rather than explaining every rule at once.
- Keep the reader's understanding aligned with what Eric has actually
  learned.

11. Open decisions for later planning
These points are deliberately left open. Decide them when the relevant
chapters or next arc are developed.
- Final names, appearance, and personalities of the mage unit members.
- Exact size and load distribution of the unit's expedition pack.
- Whether the first deployment is a routine hunt, investigation,
  escort, or response to a specific incident.
- The exact mechanics and presentation of the magical compatibility
  test.
- Whether the provisional monster names and abilities remain
  unchanged.
- How much of the monster threat is shown directly versus through
  reports and aftermath.
- The exact timing and method of Derrin's transition into specialized
  training.
- The detailed rules of mana and techniques, which should be
  maintained in their dedicated lore files.
Continuity principle: Preserve the distinction between what Eric
knows, what he suspects, what the reader knows, and what the world's
magic system actually allows. His progress should be gradual and earned.
