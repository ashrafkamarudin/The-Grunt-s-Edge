Next morning I wake up before the camp really stirs, lying there for a while just listening to the low early noise outside the tent, waiting out of habit for that familiar pressure in my ribs to show up the moment I breathe too deep.

It doesn’t.

I press lightly anyway, just to confirm it isn’t some delayed thing my body hasn’t decided to complain about yet, but there’s nothing sharp there, nothing pulling back at me, only the ordinary stiffness of sleeping on hard ground.

Magic actually fixed it.

The thought comes and goes without settling properly. It still feels like something I witnessed rather than something that happened to me, like my body is supposed to disagree with it but can’t find a reason to.

I stay still a bit longer with my hand resting where the injury used to be, then let it drop and sit up.

No point staying here.

I push the flap open and step outside.

The morning air is the same as always, damp and heavy with smoke from early fires, the kind that never fully clears because nothing here ever fully dries. The ground underfoot is already broken down into a mix of mud and packed dirt, shaped by constant movement rather than weather.

I don’t dwell on it and just head toward the training ground.

They’re already there when I arrive, the usual group scattered into loose formation with spears in hand, waiting for someone to start them moving. Corbin looks like he’s been awake long enough to regret it. Ren stretches his shoulder once when he notices me.

*“Late,”* he says.

*“I’m here,”* I answer.

Darrin doesn’t look up, only adjusts his grip on the spear like the conversation never reached him.

Training starts without anyone deciding it should. It just becomes movement—thrust, step, recover—repeated until the body stops treating it as instruction and starts treating it as rhythm.

By the time we finish, sweat has already dried on my back under the early sun, and Corbin is the first to break away, tossing his spear onto the rack like the rest of the day no longer concerns him.

---

It’s still morning when training finally winds down, so I head to the mess hall for something quick to eat before making my way straight to the medical tent.

The tent is already active when I arrive, but not like yesterday. Less chaos. More rhythm to it now, like everyone has already accepted what kind of day it’s going to be. There’s another attendant inside today, someone who moves quickly when spoken to without needing to be told twice.

The healer notices me the moment I step in.

*“Back,”* he says, like I’m just another tool returning to the table.

*“Yeah.”*

A short grunt in response. Not surprised. Just noting it.

*“Cloth is there. Water’s low, don’t waste it.”*

I don’t wait for anything else. There isn’t anything else to wait for.

The work starts the same way it did yesterday—hands moving before thought catches up. Cloth passed, pressure applied, bandages wrapped and adjusted, bodies held steady long enough for things not to get worse. After a while it stops feeling like separate tasks and turns into one continuous motion I just stay inside.

The healer doesn’t talk much while he works. Only when something needs correcting.

*“Too loose.”*

*“Tighter.”*

*“Don’t let it slip.”*

That’s it. No explanations unless I mess something up badly enough that it matters.

At one point I notice him watching me a little longer than usual.

*“You learn fast,”* he says.

I hesitate for a moment, then shake my head slightly.

*“I’m just copying what you do.”*

A short pause.

*“That’s still learning.”*

He doesn’t expand on it. Doesn’t need to.

No praise. No weight behind the words. Just a fact stated and left where it is.

The rest of the work blends together after that—checking bandages that were set the day before, replacing cloth that’s already soaked through, making sure nothing is slipping backward into worse condition. The smell of iron and damp fabric never really leaves the space, but it stops feeling sharp after a while. It just becomes part of the air.

By late afternoon, the healer finally steps back for a moment, rolling his shoulders once as if remembering he still has a body attached to him. As work slows down, I decided to excuse myself and head back to my quarters.

---

Evening training is the same rhythm as always. Corbin, Darrin, Ren—spears again, movements tighter now, more controlled, less like learning and more like repeating something already proven to work.

Nobody talks much. There isn’t really anything to say that wouldn’t break the rhythm.

When it ends, we just lean back on the dirt and let our breathing settle on its own. The camp around us is already shifting again, that slow in-between state where people are still moving but nothing urgent is left to do.

I sit there longer than I should, staring at nothing in particular, thinking about how normal today felt.

*“Hey.”*

Ren’s voice cuts in easily, like he’s been sitting on that moment and waiting for the silence to get heavy enough to step into it. He’s off to the side, lazily wiping sweat off his forearm, posture loose in a way that makes it feel like the day never really weighs on him for long.

*“I had a suspicion during morning training, but I’m pretty sure now.”*

He tilts his head slightly, eyes narrowing just a bit, not accusing, just watching.

*“Your ribs are fine now?”*

I blink once and nod.

*“Yeah. They’re fine.”*

I shift my shoulders out of habit, like I’m checking for something that isn’t there anymore, then stop myself. There’s nothing to find.

Ren exhales through his nose, faint amusement there.

*“Yeah… you were moving like someone who wasn’t protecting anything anymore.”*

It lands a little too accurately.

Darrin, who’s been quiet until now, finally speaks without looking up from his spear.

*“You stopped hesitating on your left side.”*

I glance at him.

*“…didn’t think it was that obvious.”*

*“It is,”* he says, simple, like it’s just a fact he noticed and filed away.

Ren leans back on his palms, looking between us.

*“So what, they finally fixed you properly in the tent?”*

I hesitate, then decide there’s no point dressing it up.

*“I’ve been helping there.”*

That gets Ren’s attention properly.

*“Helping how?”*

*“Carrying things. Bandaging. Holding people still. Whatever they needed.”* I pause, then add, *“I just kept going back after training. They were short on hands… and I wanted to see how the healing works.”*

Darrin’s gaze shifts slightly.

*“How it works?”*

*“The magic,”* I say. Then correct myself a little, quieter, *“Healing magic.”*

That earns a faint reaction from him, not surprise exactly, more like interest settling in.

Ren lets out a short laugh under his breath.

*“So you traded spear practice for watching people get patched up?”*

*“I still trained,”* I answer. *“Just did both.”*

Darrin nods once, like that’s enough explanation.

*“And they used magic on you?”* he asks after a moment, tone steady. *“For helping?”*

*“Yeah.”*

A quiet stretch follows that, the kind that doesn’t feel uncomfortable, just full enough that nobody rushes to break it.

Ren is the first to move again, rolling his shoulders as he pushes himself up.

*“Honestly,”* he says, *“that’s the most Fort Lowen thing I’ve heard all week.”*

Darrin huffs once, barely there, almost a laugh.

*“Seems fair,”* he adds. *“You work, you get fixed.”*

I let that sit for a moment longer than I probably need to.

Ren stretches his neck, then looks down at us like the conversation has naturally reached its end point.

*“Anyway,”* he says, tone shifting lighter again, *“let’s go get food. I’m starving.”*

We move together toward the mess hall without much urgency. The line is long, full of men eating like they’re trying to make up for the parts of the day they lost. The food is simple, filling enough to stop the edge of hunger from getting annoying, and nobody lingers longer than they need to.

Ren talks a bit while we eat, nothing important, just filling the space so it doesn’t sit empty. Darrin listens more than he speaks, only chiming in once or twice when something catches his attention. I mostly just eat and let the noise wash past.

By the time we finish, the camp outside has already started settling into night routines again. Fires being adjusted, gear being put away, voices dropping lower as the day finally runs out of things to demand.

We walk back together without saying much.

The tent feels familiar now when I duck inside.

One by one, we settle in—no conversation needed, just the quiet understanding that tomorrow will be the same shape as today until it isn’t.

I lie down last, staring up at the fabric ceiling for a while, listening to the camp slow itself down outside. My thoughts drift, not far, just enough to lose shape until I fell asleep.

---

I was suddenly woken by a loud noise.

The warning bell.

For a moment I just lie there, not fully understanding it, and then my body moves before my thoughts can properly catch up. I’m already sitting up when the fact of it fully lands.

Outside the tent flap, Corbin is already standing, looking out.

He doesn’t turn immediately. Just watches.

Then he steps back inside.

*“Up. Now.”*

No panic in his voice. Just the kind of urgency that assumes you’re already behind.

The tent breaks into motion.

Derrin is up first, moving straight to Renn and Wells and shaking them awake without hesitation before turning to his gear like he never stopped planning the next step. The veterans are already halfway dressed, hands moving through familiar routines without needing to think about them.

Mila is crouched by her boots, finishing the last lace with quick, practiced pulls. Sutter is already pulling his armor into place, steady and unhurried, like nothing here is new. Larris moves slower on one side, careful with his leg, while Gren rolls his shoulder once like he’s trying to convince it to cooperate.

I don’t wait.

I get dressed.

Everything is familiar enough now that I don’t have to think through it anymore. Strap, tighten, adjust. Movements learned through repetition until they stop feeling like choices.

By the time I step outside, I’m already part of the flow.

Corbin waits just long enough for everyone to be out.

Then we run.

The camp is already awake in the wrong way. Not organized. Not ready. Just movement everywhere at once, people crossing paths without seeing each other properly, half-armored, weapons grabbed mid-motion. The kind of chaos that only happens when something has already gone wrong.

We cut through it.

Corbin doesn’t slow until the wall is close enough that everything shifts.

*“Monster attacks… again. Only two days. No idea what type yet. Doesn’t matter.”*

A short breath, then—

*“Don’t panic. We’ve trained for this.”*

We turn a corner.

The line is already forming ahead.

Shield walls assembling unevenly before snapping into shape as veterans correct spacing by habit alone. Somewhere beyond them, pressure builds where the front is already engaged, unseen but heavy enough to shape everything around it.

A commander’s voice carries over the formation.

*“Hold the flank! If it breaks, we’re gone!”*

We slot into position.

Larris shifts his weight immediately, trying to take pressure off his leg without breaking stance. Gren rolls his shoulder again once, tighter this time, jaw set like ignoring it is the same as fixing it.

Mila notices both of them without looking directly.

*“Don’t push it,”* she says to Larris. Then, without pause, *“And stop favoring it like that. You’ll throw the line.”*

Larris exhales through his nose.

*“I’m fine.”*

*“You’re injured,”* she answers simply. *“Just don’t make it worse.”*

Gren lets out a short breath that almost becomes a laugh, but dies halfway out.

*“Shoulder’s just complaining,”* he mutters.

Sutter’s gaze passes over them once, calm and measuring, before settling forward again.

We form at the edge of the line, Corbin still adjusting spacing with short corrections, when I notice it first.

Movement beyond the ridge. Too many shapes at once. Wrong in how they move together.

Not a single charge. Not even a pack behaving like a pack. Just pressure pushing them forward, like something behind them is forcing them out of whatever was meant to hold them back.

More shapes appear behind them. Different sizes, different speeds, all spilling into the same direction without coordination.

Renn shifts beside me.

*“…that’s not one type.”*

Corbin sees it too.

His grip tightens on the spear.

*“Hold.”*

The line answers by bracing before anyone tells it to.
