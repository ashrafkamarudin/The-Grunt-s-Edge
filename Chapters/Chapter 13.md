# Chapter 13: The Compatibility Test

The announcement continued to occupy the barracks long after the soldier had left.

Recruits who had been preparing to sleep were now sitting upright on their bunks, talking excitedly about the possibility of becoming mages. Some were already imagining the things they could accomplish, while others seemed more interested in what a magical career might offer them.

"Imagine that. We could become proper mages," one recruit said, leaning against the wooden frame of his bunk. "No more running around carrying equipment or digging trenches."

"Who says you'll even have a magical core?" another replied.

"Why wouldn't I?"

"Because most people don't!"

The first recruit waved him off. "Just you wait. When I become a powerful mage, I'll remember who doubted me."

That earned a few laughs.

Across the room, another recruit had already taken things much further.

"I'll become a hero," he announced. "A famous one. I'll defeat monsters, save villages, and eventually marry a princess."

"Where are you going to find a princess?"

"That's the easy part. I'll just save her."

"From what? Your imagination?"

The barracks erupted in laughter.

Derrin was enjoying himself. He sat on his bunk with his arms folded, listening to the others before joining in.

"If we're making plans, I might as well become the greatest mage in the kingdom."

"Greatest mage?" Tomas looked at him skeptically. "You haven't even taken the test."

"That's why I need to start planning early."

"You're getting ahead of yourself."

"Someone has to have ambition."

Eric sat nearby, listening to their conversation. He smiled at some of the jokes, but his attention kept drifting back to the announcement.

A test for magical aptitude.

Magic.

The words still felt strange to him.

*Magic actually exists?*

He had been in this world for some time now. He had seen strange creatures, experienced things that would have been impossible back on Earth, and slowly adapted to a life he had never expected to live. Yet somehow, the existence of magic had never properly crossed his mind.

He had assumed there were monsters. He had seen them. But magic was something else entirely.

Could people really cast spells? Were there people who could control fire or summon lightning? Could they manipulate the elements, heal wounds, or do things that would have been considered impossible in his old world?

His thoughts wandered further.

*If magic exists, could it help me get back to Earth?*

He paused at that thought.

He had spent so much time thinking about surviving in this unfamiliar world that he rarely considered what returning home would actually mean. He missed the conveniences of his old life, the people he knew, and the things he had taken for granted. But after everything that had happened, he wasn't even sure what he would do if given the choice.

*Would I even want to return?*

He had no answer.

And then another thought occurred to him.

*Wait. What if I actually have the aptitude for magic?*

It was an odd possibility, but one that he couldn't help entertaining. He had no idea how magical talent worked or whether it was something people were born with. Perhaps tomorrow he would discover that he had a talent he never knew existed.

"Eric."

He looked up. Derrin was watching him.

"What would you do if you became a mage?"

Eric blinked. "Me?"

"Who else? We're all making plans. What about you?"

He took a moment to consider the question.

"I haven't really thought about it."

"Come on. Surely you'd do something interesting."

"Maybe I'll learn how to shoot fires."

Derrin grinned. "That's it? You could aim higher than that."

"What about you? You were just talking about becoming the greatest mage in the kingdom."

"Exactly. Someone has to set an example."

Tomas shook his head from the next bunk. "You two are getting carried away. The test hasn't even started, and you're already talking about becoming famous."

"Let us dream a little," Derrin replied.

"Dream all you want. Just don't be disappointed when none of us has a magical core."

A few recruits laughed, though some looked less amused.

"How common are magical cores anyway?" Eric asked.

Tomas shrugged. "I don't know. But I doubt they're as common as ordinary people. If they were, there'd be a lot more mages around."

"That's true," another recruit added. "Still, there's no harm in hoping."

The conversation continued for a while, eventually drifting toward other topics as the recruits settled down for the night.

Eric lay on his bunk, staring at the wooden ceiling above him.

His mind was still occupied with the announcement. He began wondering what else might exist in this world. Monsters were real, magic was real, so perhaps there were other creatures he had only ever encountered in stories.

Dragons, for example.

He had never seen one, but considering what he had already experienced, the idea no longer seemed particularly absurd.

*What else is out there?*

Eventually, exhaustion caught up with him. His thoughts became less coherent, and he drifted off to sleep.

***

The following morning, the barracks was considerably quieter.

The excitement from the previous night had not disappeared, but the recruits seemed more conscious of what awaited them. There was less boasting and fewer elaborate plans for their imagined futures. Instead, they went about their morning routine with occasional glances and whispered conversations about the assessment.

Even Derrin seemed a little more reserved.

"You nervous?" Eric asked while they were preparing for breakfast.

"Not really," Derrin replied.

Eric gave him a look.

"Fine. Maybe a little."

"Thought you were going to become the greatest mage in the kingdom."

"I still am. I'm just waiting for the test to confirm it."

Eric chuckled.

Tomas, who was standing nearby, shook his head. "You should be more worried about becoming the greatest disappointment in the kingdom."

"That's a terrible title. I'll leave it to you."

Their conversation ended when a soldier entered the barracks and ordered everyone to finish their breakfast and prepare for assembly.

The recruits quickly gathered their belongings and followed the instructions.

As they made their way toward the training grounds, Eric noticed several groups of soldiers directing people toward the same area. Recruits from other barracks were also being gathered, forming a much larger crowd than he had expected.

The assessment was not limited to their barracks. It appeared that recruits from three different barracks were participating.

By the time they arrived, approximately seventy-two recruits had assembled on the open training grounds.

The winter air was cold, and patches of snow remained scattered across the ground. Soldiers moved between the groups, organizing the recruits into orderly rows and making sure everyone remained in place.

At the far end of the training grounds, several people were preparing equipment on a long table.

Eric noticed them almost immediately.

They weren't ordinary soldiers. Their clothing was different, and the equipment they carried looked unfamiliar. A few wore robes beneath their winter cloaks, while others dressed in practical military attire. One of them was inspecting a small object held in his hand, while another spoke with an officer.

Mages.

Eric found himself watching them more closely than he intended.

He had seen soldiers wield swords, spears, and bows. He had watched them train and had a general understanding of how they fought. But these people belonged to something he had only just learned existed.

He wondered what they could do.

One of the mages raised his hand toward the equipment on the table. A faint glow appeared around his fingers, and a small metal instrument shifted slightly before settling into place.

Eric stared.

It was a simple display, barely noticeable to anyone familiar with magic. But to him, it was the first undeniable demonstration that the stories he had once considered fantasy were part of everyday life here.

"Interesting, isn't it?"

Eric turned toward Derrin.

"Yeah," he replied. "I suppose it is."

Derrin continued watching the mages with considerable interest. "I wonder how the test works."

"Hopefully they'll explain it."

Before long, an officer stepped forward and called for everyone's attention.

The conversations gradually died down.

"Today's assessment is intended to identify recruits with magical aptitude," he began. "You will be tested for the presence of a magical core. Those who possess one may be considered for further magical training, depending on the results."

He paused, allowing the recruits to absorb the information.

"Having a magical core does not automatically make you a capable mage. Your aptitude determines the potential you may develop, but training, discipline, and experience remain necessary. Some cores are weak, while others are stronger. The assessment will determine where you stand."

The officer gestured toward the table.

"The procedure is simple. Follow the assessors' instructions, remain orderly, and do not interfere with the equipment. You will each be tested individually."

One of the mages stepped forward and briefly demonstrated the process.

On the table sat a number of clear crystals mounted on small metal stands. Each was about the size of a fist, with a smooth surface that reflected the pale winter sunlight.

The mage placed his hand over one of them.

For a moment, nothing happened. Then a faint light appeared within the crystal, spreading from its center toward the edges.

"This instrument responds to the presence of a magical core," the mage explained. "The strength of the response helps us assess the recruit's aptitude."

He removed his hand, and the light gradually faded.

"Those without a magical core will produce no response. Those with one will produce a response corresponding to their aptitude."

It sounded straightforward enough, although Eric still had several questions. He wondered whether the crystal was detecting mana itself or something specific to the core. He also wondered how accurate the readings were.

Unfortunately, there wasn't much opportunity to ask.

The assessment began shortly afterward.

The recruits were called forward in small groups, with each person taking a turn before the assessors. The process moved at a steady pace. A recruit would place a hand near the crystal, wait for the response, and receive the result from the examiner.

Some crystals remained completely dark.

Others produced a faint glow.

A few shone more brightly, drawing quiet reactions from the surrounding recruits.

Eric watched as one recruit received a weak result. The examiner explained that he possessed a magical core, but its strength was limited. The recruit seemed uncertain whether he should be happy or disappointed.

Another received an average result and was told to remain nearby for further instructions.

The process continued.

As the assessment progressed, the atmosphere changed. The initial excitement gave way to a mixture of nervousness and anticipation. Some recruits began whispering about their results, while others tried to hide their disappointment.

Eric found himself paying close attention to every assessment.

He wanted to understand how the crystal worked, but he also couldn't deny that he was becoming increasingly nervous about his own turn.

Eventually, Derrin's name was called.

"Good luck," Eric said.

Derrin gave him a confident grin. "You'll see."

He stepped forward and stood before the assessor.

Following the instructions, he placed his hand near the crystal.

A few seconds passed.

Then a soft light appeared inside it.

The glow was steady, spreading throughout much of the crystal without becoming particularly intense.

The assessor examined the response before giving his verdict.

"Average magical core."

Derrin stared at him.

"Average?"

"That's right. You have sufficient aptitude to be considered for specialized mage training. Remain with the other selected recruits after the assessment."

For a moment, Derrin seemed unable to process the words. Then his face broke into an enormous grin.

"WOOOOHOOO!"

Several recruits turned toward him.

"I'M AVERAGE!" Derrin shouted, throwing both hands into the air. "DID YOU HEAR THAT? I'M AVERAGE!"

His celebration was cut short by a sharp voice.

"Recruit!"

A nearby soldier stepped forward, giving him a stern look.

"You're in a military formation, not a marketplace. Control yourself!"

Derrin immediately straightened.

"Yes, sir!"

"Keep your voice down. You can celebrate after the assessment is over. Understood?"

"Yes, sir!"

The soldier gave him one last look before returning to his position.

Derrin lowered his hands, but the enormous grin remained firmly on his face.

Eric couldn't help laughing.

"Congratulations," he said.

"Thank you," Derrin replied, still struggling to contain his excitement. "Did you hear that? Average! I got an average core!"

"Yes, I heard you. I think the entire fortress heard you."

Tomas shook his head. "You were just told to keep your voice down."

"I am keeping it down."

"You're practically vibrating."

Derrin ignored him.

Eric smiled. He was genuinely happy for his friend. An average magical core was already an incredible result for an ordinary commoner. It meant Derrin had an opportunity that very few people ever received.

"Looks like you'll have to work hard now," Eric said.

"Of course. I'll make the most of it."

"Try not to get scolded before you even begin your training."

Derrin laughed.

The assessment continued, and more names were called.

Eric watched as the remaining recruits took their turns. Some received no response, while others produced varying degrees of light. The selected recruits were gradually gathered to one side.

Eventually, the crowd became smaller, and the assessment continued with those who had yet to be tested.

Then an examiner called Eric's name.

He stepped forward.

The crystal sat on the metal stand, its clear surface reflecting the grey winter sky.

"Place your hand near the crystal," the examiner instructed.

Eric followed the direction.

He waited.

Nothing happened.

He remained still for a few more seconds, wondering whether he was supposed to do something else.

The examiner looked at the crystal, then at him.

"No magical core."

The words were delivered plainly, without any particular emotion.

Eric withdrew his hand.

"Understood."

The examiner moved on to the next recruit.

Eric stepped away from the table and returned to the others. There was no dramatic reaction from him, nor did he feel the need to say anything. The result was simple enough.

He had no magical aptitude.

***

Once the final recruits had been assessed, the officers began organizing those who had produced a positive result.

The selected recruits were instructed to remain behind for further evaluation and information about their next steps. Everyone else was dismissed.

Eric stood among the departing recruits, looking toward the group that had been asked to stay.

Derrin was there, along with several others from the three barracks. They were listening to one of the mages, who appeared to be explaining something about their upcoming training.

Derrin noticed Eric looking in his direction and raised a hand.

Eric returned the gesture.

He was glad his friend had made it. There was probably a lot he would learn from the mages, and Eric was curious about what would happen next.

For now, though, there wasn't much reason to remain.

He turned and began walking away from the training grounds.

The other recruits were already heading back toward their barracks. Some were discussing their results, while others seemed content to leave the subject alone. A few were visibly disappointed, but the conversations gradually became quieter as the group dispersed.

Eric walked a little more slowly than the others.

He eventually found himself near a section of the fortress wall, away from the busier parts of the training grounds. There was a small stretch of cleared ground beside it, where the wind was less exposed and the noise from the assessment had faded into the distance.

He sat down.

For a while, he simply looked out across the snow-covered landscape.

He could still see the distant figures gathered near the testing equipment. The selected recruits were being given further instructions, and he could just make out Derrin among them.

Eric was happy for him. There was no doubt about that.

But he couldn't quite shake the feeling that something was missing.

He had known the result was possible. Not everyone could become a mage, and there had never been any reason to believe he would be particularly gifted. He had never used magic, never felt mana, and hadn't even known such things existed until recently.

Yet hearing the examiner confirm that he had no magical core had left him feeling strangely empty.

It was a little ridiculous when he thought about it. He had only learned about magic yesterday. Before that, becoming a mage hadn't even been something he could imagine. Still, after hearing about the assessment, he had found himself wondering what it would be like to possess such an ability.

Perhaps he had been more hopeful than he realized.

Magic was something entirely new to him. Back on Earth, it belonged in books, games, and films. Here, it was real. People could actually use it, and there were people whose entire lives revolved around learning and mastering it.

For a brief moment, he had imagined himself becoming one of them.

He had even wondered whether magic might help him understand how he had ended up in this world, or perhaps give him some way to return home.

Now, that possibility seemed a little further away.

He lowered his gaze toward the snow near his boots.

*I never had magic to begin with anyway.*

It was true. He hadn't lost something he once possessed. He had simply discovered that he didn't have something he had briefly hoped for.

That didn't make the disappointment disappear, though.

After sitting quietly for a while, Eric suddenly let out a small chuckle.

*Wait a minute. I'm the one who got transported to another world.*

He shook his head, amused by his own thoughts.

*If all those stories I used to read are anything to go by, shouldn't I be the one with some ridiculously powerful magical aptitude?*

He almost laughed again.

*Wouldn't that be convenient?*

Apparently, reality had other plans. He was just an ordinary guy who had somehow ended up in another world, and not even magic seemed particularly interested in cooperating with him.

The thought was ridiculous enough to make him smile.

But once the amusement faded, his thoughts gradually returned to magic itself.

What exactly was it?

He had seen the crystal respond to the presence of a magical core, but that only raised more questions. Was mana a tangible substance that existed around them, even though it couldn't be seen? Was it something like the air, present everywhere but only noticeable under certain conditions?

Or was it closer to energy?

He thought about electricity. It was invisible too, yet it could be measured, stored, and used to power all sorts of things. Perhaps mana worked in a similar way, although he had no idea whether the comparison made any sense.

And what about the magical core? Did it produce mana, store it, or simply allow someone to interact with it?

He leaned back slightly, looking toward the pale sky.

There were so many things he didn't know.

The assessment had given him one answer, at least. He had no magical core. But it hadn't explained how magic actually worked, and he was beginning to realize just how little he understood about the world around him.

He let out a quiet breath and brushed his hands together.

There wasn't much he could do with those questions for now. He would need to learn more before he could make sense of any of it.

After sitting for a little longer, Eric finally stood and brushed the snow from his trousers. He glanced once more toward the training grounds, where the selected recruits were still gathered, before turning back toward the barracks.

He would have to ask someone about magic eventually.

For now, there were other things he needed to focus on.

***

The following days returned to their usual routine.

The magical aptitude assessment had been completed, and the recruits resumed their physical training. Those selected for specialized mage training were gradually separated from the others as the army began arranging their next assignments.

Derrin was among them.

Their schedules would soon become different, though they still found opportunities to speak whenever their routines allowed. Derrin occasionally shared small details about the instructions he had received, while Eric continued with the regular training program.

There was plenty to keep him occupied.

Running, strength exercises, marching, and carrying equipment remained part of their daily routine. The winter conditions made everything more demanding, particularly when the snow had not been cleared from the training grounds.

Eric, however, had begun to notice how much he had improved.

The long runs that once left him struggling near the back of the group were becoming more manageable. He could maintain his pace for longer distances, and his breathing recovered more quickly after strenuous exercises.

His strength had also improved. Loads that had once felt particularly heavy were now easier to manage, and he could complete more repetitions during the various bodyweight exercises.

He wasn't the strongest recruit, nor was he the fastest. There were still several commoners who could outperform him in particular exercises, and some had natural advantages that he couldn't easily match.

Nevertheless, Eric had become one of the better physical performers among the commoner recruits.

His progress hadn't happened overnight. It was the result of weeks of consistent effort, pushing himself through exhausting exercises and gradually adapting to the demands of military training.

He had also begun incorporating his own exercises whenever he had the time. Nothing particularly complicated, just additional work to improve his strength and endurance.

The results were starting to show.

Even so, he knew he still had a long way to go. Being physically fit was one thing. Being able to fight and survive against the monsters outside the fortress was another entirely.

For now, he would continue training and improving wherever he could.

Magic remained a subject he was curious about, and perhaps he would find an opportunity to learn more someday.

But until then, there was still plenty of work to do.
