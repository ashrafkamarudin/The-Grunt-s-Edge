# Chapter 10: Grayford

The training station looked larger as they approached.

From the road, Eric could make out more than he had seen from the hillside. The timber fence surrounded a broad stretch of ground, with several long buildings arranged around an open yard. A storehouse stood near the rear, and smaller structures lined one side of the enclosure. Smoke rose from a few chimneys, carried away by the cool afternoon breeze.

It was not a fortress. There were no great stone walls or towering gates. But it was clearly built for a purpose.

Men moved within the enclosure. Some carried bundles between the buildings, while others crossed the yard in groups. Near one end, several soldiers were practising with wooden weapons. Their movements were sharp and repetitive, accompanied by the occasional barked instruction.

Eric watched them as the column drew closer.

This was where they would be staying.

The soldiers leading the column called for them to close their ranks. The recruits shuffled into position, tired legs carrying them through the final stretch.

At the gate, a guard stepped forward and raised his hand.

"Column halt!"

The order travelled down the line. Boots scraped against the road as the recruits stopped.

A captain approached from inside the enclosure. He spoke briefly with the soldier leading the column, then looked over the gathered men.

"These are the new arrivals?"

"Yes, sir. Recruits from the surrounding settlements. Two days on the road."

The captain's eyes moved across them. He took in the packs, shields, armour and tired faces.

"Bring them inside. Keep the column together. No wandering."

The gate was opened.

The recruits were led into the station.

The difference was immediate.

Outside, the road had been quiet, with only the occasional settlement or passing traveller. Inside the enclosure, there was constant movement. Soldiers crossed between buildings carrying equipment. A group of men marched across the far side of the yard, their boots striking the ground in a steady rhythm. Somewhere nearby, a hammer struck metal.

A voice shouted from the training yard.

"Again! Your shield is too low!"

Wooden weapons cracked against one another.

Eric turned his head towards the sound.

"Eyes front!" a soldier called.

He quickly looked ahead.

The column was directed towards a long building beside the yard. A soldier ordered them to place their packs against the wall and leave their shields in a neat row.

"Armour stays with you. Shields and marching equipment will be collected and stored. You will receive them again when instructed."

Men began setting down their belongings.

Eric carefully removed his shield and placed it with the others. His shoulders felt lighter immediately.

A soldier walked along the row, checking that everything had been left properly. He stopped at one recruit whose shield was leaning against another.

"Flat. Not leaning."

The recruit corrected it.

"Keep your equipment in order. You will be responsible for it."

The soldier continued down the line.

A clerk sat behind a wooden table near the entrance. Several bundles of papers lay before him, along with a small wooden box filled with tokens.

"Next."

The first recruit stepped forward.

"Name?"

The man answered.

"Age?"

"Eighteen."

"Village?"

The clerk wrote something down.

"Previous service?"

"No."

"Weapon experience?"

"None."

The questions continued. Name, age, village, previous service, weapon experience and any injuries that might affect training.

The process moved slowly. Some recruits answered quickly. Others struggled to remember details or had to repeat themselves.

When Eric's turn came, he stepped forward.

"Name?"

"Eric."

The clerk looked down at his paper.

"Age?"

"Twenty."

"Village?"

"Eastwood."

"Previous service?"

"None."

"Weapons?"

"None."

"Occupation?"

Eric paused.

"I helped with general work in the village."

The clerk glanced at him.

"What kind of work?"

"Carrying supplies, gathering wood, helping with repairs and harvest work."

The clerk made a note.

"Injuries?"

"My knee was injured before I arrived at Eastwood. It's healed enough to walk, but it still gets sore sometimes."

"Anything else?"

"My feet and shoulders are sore from the march."

The clerk looked him over, then marked something on the paper.

"Go to the medical station. They will check you before you're assigned."

He reached for a token, then paused as his eyes moved to another sheet on the table.

"One more thing. Have you been told your pay?"

Eric shook his head.

The clerk gave a small sigh, as though he had already answered the same question too many times that day.

"Fifteen Silver Marks a month while in service. Five Marks are paid out to you each month. The remaining ten are held until you complete your term."

He tapped the paper with his quill.

"Food, lodging, standard equipment and basic treatment are provided. You don't pay for those out of your wages. If you die while serving, your outstanding pay and the compensation due will be paid to your registered next of kin. Make sure the name on that record is correct."

Eric listened carefully. Fifteen Silver Marks sounded like a considerable amount compared with the small sums he had seen exchanged in Eastwood. But he would only receive a portion of it each month.

"How long is the term?" he asked.

The clerk looked at him.

"That depends on the order under which you've been called. Your term and conditions are written here. Read them when you have time. Next."

He reached into the wooden box and handed Eric a small token. A number had been carved into one side.

"Keep this. You may be asked for it. Do not lose it."

Eric took the token and stepped aside.

As he moved away from the table, something pale drifted past his face. He looked up. A small white flake descended through the air before disappearing against the dark sleeve of his tunic.

Another followed. Then another.

Snow.

Eric held out his hand. A few flakes settled against his skin, melting almost immediately. He had expected the weather to turn colder, but he hadn't thought winter would arrive so soon.

Around the yard, a few soldiers glanced towards the sky. One pulled his cloak tighter while another continued carrying a crate as if nothing had happened.

"First snow," Darrin said behind him.

Eric turned. Darrin had just finished registering and was looking up at the falling flakes.

"Looks like winter's caught up with us," he added.

Tomas stepped out from the table after him, brushing a flake from his sleeve.

"It'll probably stop soon."

"You sound disappointed."

"I'm thinking about the road we just walked."

Darrin glanced at his boots. "Fair enough."

The next recruit approached.

When they had all finished, the recruits were directed towards a smaller building at the far end of the yard.

The medical station smelled of herbs, boiled water and old wood. A few men were already waiting inside. One sat on a bench while a healer examined his ankle. Another had his hand wrapped in cloth.

Eric waited until his turn.

A man in a plain tunic asked him to sit.

"Where does it hurt?"

Eric pointed to his knee.

The man examined it, pressing gently around the joint and asking him to bend and straighten his leg. He then checked Eric's feet, looking for blisters and damaged skin.

"Nothing serious," he said. "Your feet have taken a beating, though."

He cleaned one small area where the skin had rubbed raw and wrapped it with fresh cloth.

"Keep these clean. Change the dressing when it gets dirty. If the skin breaks further, report it."

He looked at Eric's knee again.

"Don't push yourself too hard during the first few days. If it swells or becomes painful, come back."

Eric nodded.

The man marked something on a small sheet and handed it to him.

"Give this to the clerk outside."

Eric left the building.

A few recruits were waiting nearby, some sitting on a low bench while others stood around checking their belongings. Eric handed the sheet to the clerk before joining the others.

A soldier came to collect them.

"Come on. Equipment issue."

The quartermaster's building was larger than the medical station. Shelves and racks filled most of the interior, with bundles stacked along the walls. Several soldiers were sorting supplies while a quartermaster stood behind a counter.

The recruits were called forward in small groups.

"Token."

Eric handed his over.

The quartermaster checked the number against a list, then began placing items on the counter.

A heavier blanket.

Two pairs of spare socks.

A wooden bowl and cup.

A small belt pouch.

Several strips of cloth for wrapping and repairs.

The items were not new. The blanket had been mended in one corner, and the bowl showed signs of use. The pouch was plain, with a simple fastening.

"Check what you've received," the quartermaster said. "If something is missing, say so now. If you lose it later, you'll answer for it."

Eric examined the supplies.

"That's everything?"

"Everything on your list."

He stepped aside to make room for the next recruit.

The recruits continued collecting their supplies. Darrin glanced over his own pile.

"That's a lot of things to carry."

"Better than having nothing," Tomas replied.

"I didn't say it wasn't."

A soldier walked along the line, making sure the recruits had received the correct supplies.

"You'll be responsible for keeping these in usable condition. The weather will get colder before long. Keep your spare clothing dry. Don't leave your blanket outside. If you have wet socks, change them when you can."

He pointed towards the equipment.

"Your padded layers and cloaks are issued for the coming season. They are not an excuse to neglect your gear. Check your straps, repair loose stitching and report anything that cannot be fixed."

Eric looked down at the items in his hands.

The station seemed to be preparing for the same change in weather as Eastwood. The mornings were already cold, and the evenings were becoming shorter. It would not be long before the chill became something more difficult to ignore.

Outside, the first snow continued to fall. It gathered briefly along the edges of the buildings and on the wooden fence, though much of it melted against the ground.

The recruits were directed towards the barracks.

The building stood along the western side of the enclosure. It was long and plain, with narrow windows and a wide entrance. Inside, rows of wooden bunks filled the room. Each had a thin mattress, a folded blanket and a small space beneath for belongings.

The air smelled of straw, old wood and damp clothing.

A sergeant stood near the entrance with a list in his hand.

"Listen for your names. When I call you, take the assigned space. Do not change it without permission."

He began reading.

The recruits were divided between several barracks. Eric was assigned to Barracks C, in the west hall.

He found his place. It was an upper bunk, with a narrow ladder fixed to the side. He placed his belongings beneath it and set the new blanket on the mattress.

It was not comfortable, but it was better than the ground.

A moment later, Darrin came over, looking between the assigned bunks.

"Wait. You're in C too?"

Eric checked the mark on his token. "Looks like it."

Darrin's expression brightened.

"Wow, we got the same barrack!"

It was the first time since leaving the road that he seemed genuinely excited about something.

Tomas arrived shortly after, checking the spaces around them.

"Looks like I'm here too."

Eric gave a small nod. He was glad not to be surrounded entirely by strangers, though he still barely knew either of them.

Darrin climbed onto the bunk below Eric's and dropped his pack with a heavy thud.

"At least we have a roof."

Tomas placed his things neatly beside his bed.

"Don't start complaining about the roof."

"I'm not. I like the roof."

Before they could settle further, a voice sounded from the entrance.

"All recruits. Stand outside."

The men began moving.

They filed out into the yard, where several groups had already gathered. The afternoon light was fading, and a cool breeze moved between the buildings.

A sergeant stood in front of them. He was lean, with a close-cropped beard and a voice that carried easily across the yard.

"Stand straight. Close the gaps."

The recruits shuffled into position.

The sergeant walked along the front of the group, looking them over.

"Some of you have marched here. Some of you have never been farther than the next village. Some of you think being sent here makes you soldiers."

He stopped.

"It does not."

No one spoke.

"You are here because your villages sent you. That is all. You have no training. Most of you have never held a weapon properly. Some of you can barely walk after two days on the road."

A few recruits shifted their weight.

"That will change."

He turned slightly, gesturing towards the yard.

"You will learn to follow orders. You will learn to move as a group. You will learn to use your equipment and look after yourselves. You will be taught how to fight, how to defend your position and how to survive outside these walls."

His eyes moved across the recruits.

"You will not decide which lessons you attend. You will not decide when training begins or ends. You will not leave the station without permission. If you are injured, report it. If your equipment is damaged, report it. If you have a problem, bring it to the proper person."

He paused.

"Do not fight among yourselves. Do not steal. Do not enter buildings or storage areas unless you have been told to. Do not touch weapons or training equipment without permission."

His voice hardened.

"Disobey an instructor, break formation or attempt to leave, and you will be punished. I do not care whether you were a farmer, a miller's son or the strongest man in your village. Here, you follow the same rules."

He looked towards the barracks.

"First formation is at first light. You will be dressed, equipped and standing in the yard before the call. Late means extra work. Do not make me come looking for you."

He let the words settle.

"Return to your barracks. You will be shown the station before supper. Training begins tomorrow."

The sergeant dismissed them.

The recruits returned inside, though they were not given much time to rest. Another soldier arrived shortly afterwards and led them towards the mess hall.

It was a broad building with long tables and benches. A few soldiers were already eating, while others moved between the serving counter and the kitchen.

The smell of food filled the room.

Eric realised how hungry he was.

The recruits were given bowls of porridge, slices of dark bread and a small portion of dried meat. It was plain food, but warm and filling.

He sat with the others from his barracks and began eating. Darrin was already halfway through his bowl, while Tomas broke off a piece of bread.

"Better than the road," Darrin said.

Eric couldn't disagree. The long march had left him hungry, and the warmth of the meal was welcome.

A soldier walked between the tables.

"Finish your food. You'll need it tomorrow."

Darrin looked up.

"Is there more?"

The soldier glanced at him.

"If you're still hungry, you should have eaten slower."

Darrin looked down at his bowl.

Tomas hid a smile.

After supper, the recruits were led around the station.

The first stop was the latrine building, set away from the barracks. The soldier explained that they were expected to keep it clean and report problems.

Next was a bathing area, with several large wooden tubs and a place to heat water. It was not particularly comfortable, but it was better than washing in the open.

The infirmary stood nearby. Eric recognised the building from earlier.

"Any injury that affects your ability to train must be reported," the soldier said. "Do not hide it. If you make a small injury worse by ignoring it, that is your own foolishness."

They were shown the storehouse next. Its doors were closed, and a soldier stood guard outside.

"Food, clothing and other supplies are kept here. You do not enter without permission."

A smaller building held training equipment. Through the open doorway, Eric could see wooden swords, practice shields and several racks of spears. Real weapons were kept elsewhere.

The soldier did not allow them to linger.

Finally, they reached the training yard.

It was larger than Eric had expected. Wooden dummies stood in rows along one side. Practice weapons were arranged near a covered shelter, and several groups of soldiers were already working through drills.

One group moved with shields raised, stepping forward and backward in unison. Another practised strikes against wooden targets. Their instructor walked between them, correcting their positions.

"Again!" he shouted.

The group repeated the movement.

"Your feet! Watch your feet! You cannot hold a line if you don't know where to stand!"

A wooden sword struck a shield with a sharp crack.

Eric watched closely.

The movements seemed simple at first. Raise the shield. Step forward. Strike. Move back.

But the men repeated each action carefully, and the instructor corrected even small mistakes.

Eric found himself watching their feet more than their weapons. The men had to move together, each step matching the others. A single person falling out of position could leave a gap in the line.

The soldier leading them continued the tour.

They passed the main yard, where recruits would gather for formation, and an instructor's building where lessons and briefings were held. Beyond it stood a row of smaller rooms used for equipment maintenance and repairs.

The station was not large, but there was more to it than Eric had first imagined. Every building had a purpose. Every group seemed to know where it was supposed to be and what it was supposed to do.

Even the men moving supplies worked with a sense of routine.

As they crossed the yard, Eric noticed a pair of soldiers speaking beside the training shelter. One had a narrow strip of parchment in his hand.

"...the patrol from the eastern farms?"

"They've been called back in. Something's been moving closer to the road."

"Already? The cold has barely started."

"I don't know. That's not for us to decide."

Their conversation ended as an instructor called one of them over.

Eric glanced towards the fence. Beyond it, the falling snow softened the outlines of the surrounding fields. He wondered what had been moving out there, but the soldier leading the recruits was already calling them forward.

The soldier brought them back to the barracks.

"Keep your equipment in order. Do not leave belongings lying around. You will have time to wash and settle before lights out. First formation is at dawn."

He looked across the group.

"Tomorrow will be harder than today."

Then he left.

The barracks gradually filled with the sounds of men settling in. Some spoke quietly, others checked their equipment or examined the new supplies. A few had already climbed into their bunks.

Eric sat on the edge of his mattress and sorted through his belongings.

He placed the spare socks beneath his blanket, folded his cloak and checked the straps on his armour. The knife Lena had given him was still secure in its pouch. He set it within reach.

Darrin was struggling with one of his armour straps.

"How does this thing go?"

Tomas glanced over.

"Which part?"

"This."

Tomas came closer, examined the strap and adjusted it.

"You're pulling it the wrong way."

Darrin watched carefully.

"That's all?"

"That's all."

"Could have told me earlier."

"You didn't ask."

Darrin shook his head, but there was a smile on his face.

Tomas returned to his bunk and arranged his belongings. He sat down, turning the wooden pendant in his fingers.

"Are you nervous?" Darrin asked him.

Tomas looked up.

"A little."

"Good. Thought it was just me."

Darrin leaned back against the wall.

"I wonder what they'll make us do first."

"Probably something unpleasant," Tomas said.

"That's encouraging."

A soldier entered the barracks.

"Half an hour until lights out. Finish what you're doing."

The men began settling down.

Eric looked towards the narrow window.

The light outside was fading. Beyond the fence, the training yard was quieter now, though a few soldiers were still moving equipment back into storage.

The air had grown colder as evening approached. The season was changing, and the station seemed prepared for it. Blankets had been issued, spare clothing distributed and supplies arranged for the months ahead.

Eastwood had been doing much the same before he left. Stacking wood, storing food, repairing roofs and preparing for the cold.

Here, the preparations were larger, but the purpose was familiar.

He thought of Kael and Lena for a moment. He wondered what they were doing now, whether the evening fire had been lit and whether the cold had begun to settle into their house.

He pushed the thought aside.

He was here now.

Tomorrow, he would begin learning how to fight.

Not because he wanted to serve the Empire. Not because he had any particular desire to become a soldier.

He simply did not want to be helpless again.

The memory of the creature from his childhood remained buried somewhere in his mind. He could not remember everything about it, but he remembered the fear. He remembered being unable to do anything.

He had spent years in a world where such things were impossible, or at least distant enough to feel unreal. Now he was back in a place where monsters existed beyond the safety of village walls.

The soldiers' brief conversation returned to him. Reports from the farms. Something moving closer to the road. He did not know whether it was connected to the monsters he remembered, or whether it was simply another part of life in this world.

But the first snow had come earlier than he expected, and the men here seemed to have more to worry about than the changing weather.

If he wanted to survive, he needed to learn.

He looked at his hands. They were still rough from the march, with small scratches and marks from the work he had done in Eastwood.

They were not the hands of a fighter.

Not yet.

Eric climbed onto his bunk and pulled the blanket over himself. The mattress was thin, and the building was not especially warm, but it offered shelter from the wind.

Around him, conversations gradually faded. Someone coughed. A bunk creaked. From outside came the distant sound of a door closing.

Then the sergeant's voice carried through the hall.

"Lights out!"

The lamps were extinguished.

The barracks fell into darkness.

Eric lay still, listening to the unfamiliar sounds around him. The march was over. The road behind him was gone, and the training station had become his new home, at least for a while.

Tomorrow, the training would begin.
