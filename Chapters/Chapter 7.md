# Chapter 7: The King's Due

The communal building was more crowded than Eric had ever seen it.

Villagers stood shoulder to shoulder around the long tables. Some still wore their work clothes, with dirt on their boots and sleeves. A few children lingered near their parents, whispering and peeking towards the soldiers waiting outside.

Eric stood beside Kael, with Lena just behind them.

At the front, the elder held the sealed parchment the soldiers had delivered that morning. He waited for the room to settle before breaking the silence.

"We've received an order from the Empire," he began. "Eastwood is to provide one able-bodied man for the Imperial Army. He must be at Greybridge by the third morning from today."

A farmer near the back frowned.

"One man? That's all?"

"That's what it says."

"Then we ought to count ourselves lucky," someone muttered.

A few people gave dry laughs.

The elder glanced down at the parchment again.

"There's another part. If we can't provide a man, we can pay in grain or other goods instead."

"How much?"

The elder hesitated.

"Quite a lot."

"How much?" the farmer repeated.

The elder read out the amount.

Voices rose at once.

"That's half our grain!"

"Half? More than that!"

"We'd be starving before spring."

"Then what are we supposed to do?"

The elder raised a hand, trying to quiet them.

"There's more. If we provide neither a man nor the goods, the officials can choose someone themselves."

A man near the doorway spat onto the floor.

"So they'll take someone either way."

"That's what the order says," the elder replied.

The room fell quiet.

Eric looked around at the faces. He knew most of these people now. They had worked beside him, shared meals with him, and stopped to talk whenever they crossed paths.

The Empire wanted a man. If the village couldn't provide one, it wanted a large share of their food. And if they couldn't manage that, someone would be chosen for them.

***

The arguments started again almost immediately.

"Send grain," one farmer said. "Better that than losing a man."

"With what grain?" another snapped. "You got sacks hidden away somewhere? Because I sure as hell don't."

"We could ask the next village."

"They've got their own troubles. You think they'll hand over their winter food?"

"Then ask the lord to lower the amount."

Aldric shook his head. "We can ask. Doesn't mean he'll agree."

A farmer turned on him.

"You're always talking to the lord's men. Can't you do something for once?"

"I'll speak to them. I can't make them listen."

"Then what's the point?"

"Enough," another villager called. "He's not the one who made the order!"

The farmer grumbled but turned away.

A woman near the table spoke up.

"My family barely has enough to last the winter. You want us to give away more?"

"We could offer some animals," someone suggested.

"And what do we eat next year? The fence posts?"

"Not all of them, you fool. A few."

"Every animal's worth something come winter."

The voices began to overlap. Every suggestion seemed to leave someone worse off.

Eric leaned closer to Kael.

"Can they really demand that much?"

"They can ask."

"And if we refuse?"

"Then we send a man."

Kael's answer was quiet, but Eric could tell he wasn't happy about it.

Aldric stepped forward.

"We can't give them that much grain. Everyone here knows how poor the harvest was. Some of us barely have enough for our own families."

"Then send someone," a man said.

Nobody answered him.

The elder set the parchment down on the table.

"Before we start naming people, is anyone willing to volunteer?"

Silence.

A farmer eventually cleared his throat.

"My eldest could go."

His wife turned sharply towards him.

"Your eldest? Who's going to help you with the fields, then?"

"I know what I'm saying."

"And what about your youngest? You expect him to do it all?"

The farmer looked down.

"I said I know."

Nobody seemed eager to press him further.

Another man spoke up.

"What about the miller's nephew? He's eighteen."

"His uncle needs him."

"He's got two other workers."

"One can barely lift a bloody sack!"

"Then what about Silas?"

Aldric looked towards the speaker.

"His mother depends on him."

"He's strong enough. Eighteen, isn't he?"

"He's a lazy sod half the time," someone else said.

"Well, he's not the only one."

A few people snorted.

The discussion grew louder. Names were thrown around, then dismissed. Too young, too old, needed at home, injured, or simply not suited for army service.

There weren't many young men in Eastwood to begin with. And those who were old enough were already helping their families get through the winter.

Then Aldric spoke.

"What about Eric?"

The room quieted.

Eric looked up, unsure whether he'd heard correctly.

"Vane," Aldric added, looking towards him.

"Eric," Kael corrected.

Aldric glanced at him, then nodded.

"Eric, then. He's young, healthy, and strong enough to serve. He has no land, no wife, no children. No one here depends on him."

Several villagers shifted uncomfortably.

"He's been working harder than some of us," a farmer said.

"That's not what I'm saying."

"He helped us with the harvest. Carried timber for the repairs, too."

"And helped me with the firewood," another added.

Aldric nodded. "I know he's helped. I'm not saying otherwise."

"Then why are you talking like he's got nothing to lose?"

Aldric's expression tightened.

"Because the rest of us have families to feed. Some of us can't manage the work without our strongest men. We have to think about the village."

Kael stepped forward.

"Eric isn't a spare pair of hands you can hand over because he has no land."

"I didn't say he was."

"You said he could leave without putting anyone in trouble."

"Is that not true?"

Kael stared at him for a moment.

"He's worked here. He's helped the people in this room. We've shared our food with him, and he's been part of our lives these past weeks. Don't talk as if he has no place here."

Aldric's jaw tightened.

"Having a place here doesn't change what the Empire demands."

"No. It doesn't."

Lena stepped out from behind Kael.

"He helped us."

Her voice wasn't loud, but several people turned towards her.

"He helped Father with the wood. He worked at the communal building. People ask him for help because they know he'll do it."

She looked straight at Aldric.

"Why are we talking about him like he doesn't belong here?"

Aldric was quiet for a moment.

"Because belonging here and being able to stay aren't always the same thing."

Lena frowned but said nothing more.

Eric stood still, feeling everyone's eyes on him.

He hadn't expected Kael or Lena to defend him. He hadn't realised how many villagers had noticed the work he'd done.

But he understood Aldric's argument, too.

The others had families. People who needed them. If one of them left, someone back home would have to carry the burden.

Eric had no family in Eastwood.

That didn't mean he had nothing to lose.

***

The discussion continued, but Eric found himself paying less attention to it.

He looked around the room. The farmer who had offered his eldest son stood beside his wife. The miller's nephew was talking quietly with his uncle. Silas's mother stood near the wall, her hands clasped together.

There was no easy choice.

Eric thought about what would happen if he refused. The villagers would keep arguing. Eventually, they would have to send someone, whether they agreed on it or the soldiers chose for them.

He didn't want to leave Eastwood.

He had only just begun to feel at home here. Kael had given him shelter, and Lena had patiently helped him with the language. The villagers had welcomed him into their work and shared what little they had.

But staying meant someone else would have to go.

Eric looked towards Kael. The older man was still watching the discussion, his expression grim.

There was another reason he considered it.

He knew almost nothing about the world beyond this village. The army would take him to other places. He might see towns, cities, and learn more about the Empire. Perhaps he could even find some clue about what had happened to his family.

It was a frightening way to leave, but it was a way forward.

He took a breath.

"I'll go."

The room fell silent.

Kael turned sharply.

"Eric."

Eric looked towards the elder.

"I'll be the one Eastwood sends."

The elder studied him.

"Do you understand what you're offering? Army service isn't a short journey. You'll have to follow orders and go wherever they send you."

Eric nodded.

"I understand."

Kael stepped closer.

"You don't have to do this."

"I know."

"Then don't do it because you think you owe us something."

"I'm not."

Kael searched his face.

Eric struggled to find the right words. He still couldn't always say what he meant, not in this language.

"The others have families," he said at last. "People who need them. I can go."

"So do you," Lena said.

Eric turned towards her.

She looked upset.

"I know."

"Then why?"

He hesitated.

"Because I can."

It wasn't the whole answer, but it was the part he could explain.

The elder looked towards Kael.

"He's volunteered."

"I heard him."

Aldric had remained silent. His arms were still folded, but he no longer looked quite so certain.

"If he's willing," he said, "then Eastwood can meet the order without taking someone from another family."

A farmer near the back shook his head.

"Doesn't make it right."

"No," Aldric replied. "It doesn't."

The elder picked up the parchment.

"Then we'll put his name forward. The soldiers still need to accept him, but this should satisfy the order."

He looked at Eric.

"Are you certain?"

Eric glanced around the room.

Some villagers looked relieved. Others avoided his eyes. The farmer whose son had been considered gave him an awkward nod.

Nobody looked happy.

Eric nodded.

"I'm certain."

***

The meeting broke apart slowly.

People left in small groups, speaking in low voices. The soldiers were informed of the village's decision. Their leader said he would take Eric's name and check him over before they departed for Greybridge.

The order still had to be fulfilled. Eric would need to be presented there by the third morning.

Aldric approached him near the doorway.

"You're making a difficult choice."

Eric wasn't sure what to say.

Aldric glanced towards the villagers leaving.

"I meant what I said earlier. We need every pair of hands we can get. You've helped more than I expected."

"I tried."

"I know."

Aldric hesitated.

"Some will be grateful. Some might feel guilty. Don't take either too much to heart."

He gave Eric a brief nod before walking away.

Kael was waiting outside with Lena.

They started walking home without speaking.

The afternoon wind moved through the bare branches along the path. Somewhere near the storehouse, a cart creaked. A child called out to someone, and the sound of a hammer carried from beyond the houses.

The village was returning to its usual work.

Lena walked a little ahead, her hands tucked into her sleeves. Kael kept pace beside Eric.

After a while, Kael spoke.

"We'll talk tonight."

Eric nodded.

There was still much to arrange. The journey, the supplies, and whatever he would need to take with him. Three days wasn't much time.

By the time they reached the house, the sun had begun to sink behind the trees.

Eastwood looked much as it always had. Small houses, narrow paths, smoke rising from chimneys. The wooden palisade stood around it, weathered and familiar.

Eric paused at the door.

He had come here with nothing.

Now, he was leaving people behind.

He followed Kael and Lena inside.
