# Chapter 7: The King's Due

The communal building was more crowded than Eric had ever seen it.

Villagers stood along the walls and gathered around the long tables, speaking in low voices. Some had come straight from their work, carrying the smell of the fields with them. Others had brought their children, who stayed close to their parents and seemed more interested in the soldiers outside than the discussion within.

The three Imperial soldiers had remained near the entrance. Their horses were tethered outside, and one of them was speaking with the watchman. They didn't appear particularly concerned by the gathering.

Eric stood beside Kael, with Lena a little behind them.

At the front of the room, the village elder held the document delivered the previous morning. He waited until the murmuring had quieted.

"We have received an order from the Imperial authorities," he began. "It concerns the settlement's obligation to provide a recruit for the army."

A few people shifted uneasily.

The elder unfolded the document.

"Eastwood is required to provide one able-bodied man for Imperial service. He must be presented at the collection point at Greybridge no later than the third morning from today."

A farmer near the back raised his voice.

"One man? That's all?"

"One," the elder confirmed.

"Then we should be grateful," someone muttered.

A few others gave humourless laughs.

The elder continued reading.

"If the settlement cannot provide a recruit, an alternative contribution in grain or other approved goods may be submitted in place of a man. The required amount is stated here. Any alternative contribution must be accepted by the officials before the deadline."

He paused, looking down at the page.

"The amount is considerable."

That silenced the room more effectively than his earlier words.

Aldric stood near the front, arms folded. "How considerable?"

The elder read out the quantity.

The reaction was immediate.

"That's half our winter stores."

"More than half, if the harvest was as poor as it was."

"We can't give them that."

"Then what are we supposed to do?"

The elder raised a hand.

"Let me finish. If neither contribution is made, the officials have the authority to select a suitable person from the settlement."

A man near the doorway shook his head.

"So they'll take someone anyway."

The elder lowered the document.

"That is what the order says."

For a moment, nobody spoke.

Eric looked around the room. He recognised most of the faces. Some of these people had worked beside him, shared meals with him, or stopped to exchange a few words when he passed through the village. They were not strangers anymore, even if he still knew very little about their lives.

He understood enough of the discussion to follow what had happened.

The Empire wanted a person.

If Eastwood couldn't provide one, it wanted a great deal of food instead.

And if the village couldn't manage either, someone would be chosen for them.

It seemed a remarkably simple way to make a difficult problem.

***

The arguments began almost at once.

"We should send grain," one of the farmers said. "Better to lose food than a man."

"With what grain?" another replied. "You think the rest of us have been hiding sacks under our beds?"

"We can ask the neighbouring settlements to help."

"They have their own orders to worry about."

"Then perhaps the lord will reduce the amount."

Aldric shook his head. "The order is already here. We can ask, but I wouldn't expect much."

The farmer turned towards him.

"You're the one who speaks with the lord's people. Can't you do something?"

"I can speak to them. I can't promise they'll listen."

"Then what good is that?"

Aldric's expression tightened, but he didn't answer.

The elder tried to bring the discussion back to the order.

"We should at least consider whether we can meet the alternative contribution."

"Consider it all you want," a woman said. "My household has barely enough grain to last until spring."

Several people murmured in agreement.

A man who kept livestock spoke next.

"We could offer animals."

"And leave ourselves without breeding stock?" someone asked. "What would we have next year?"

"Not every animal."

"Every animal is worth something when winter comes."

The conversation circled back on itself. Every suggestion seemed to take something the village needed just as much as the Empire wanted it.

Eric glanced towards Kael.

"Can they really take that much?" he asked quietly.

Kael kept his eyes on the room.

"They can ask."

"And we have to give it?"

"If we agree to the alternative and the officials accept it."

"What if we don't?"

Kael looked at him briefly.

"Then we provide a man."

Eric watched the villagers.

He had seen them preparing for winter over the past few weeks. They had stacked firewood, repaired fences, gathered the last of the harvest, and stored what food they could. None of it had looked excessive. There was no great surplus waiting to be traded away.

He could see why the grain would be a problem.

Aldric stepped forward.

"We cannot give them the amount they've asked for. Not without putting households at risk. We've already discussed the harvest. Everyone knows how much we have."

"Then we send someone," a villager said.

The words were simple.

The room fell quiet again.

***

The elder set the document on the table.

"Before we begin discussing names, I want to ask whether anyone is willing to volunteer."

Nobody moved.

Eric waited.

He had expected someone to speak. Perhaps a young man with no family, or someone who wanted to join the army. But the villagers looked at one another without saying anything.

The silence stretched.

Finally, a farmer cleared his throat.

"My eldest could go."

His wife turned towards him.

"He helps you with the fields," someone said.

"I know."

"Your youngest can't manage them alone."

"I know that too."

The farmer looked down at his hands.

His wife said nothing, but her expression had changed.

Another villager spoke.

"What about the miller's nephew? He's old enough."

"He's eighteen," someone answered. "And his uncle needs him at the mill."

"His uncle has two other workers."

"One of them can barely lift a sack."

The discussion began again, this time with names.

Each suggestion brought another objection. Some were too young. Others were too old or had injuries that made them unsuitable. Some were needed by their households. A few had families of their own.

Eric listened, slowly piecing together the village's circumstances.

There weren't many young men in Eastwood. Not many to begin with, and fewer still who could be spared without making someone else's life harder.

Then a voice from the side of the room spoke.

"What about Silas?"

Aldric looked towards the speaker.

"His mother depends on him."

"She depends on everyone. He's eighteen and strong enough."

"He's not the most reliable lad."

"Neither is he the only one with faults."

A few people exchanged glances.

Eric remembered Silas vaguely. He had seen him around the village, usually with a group of younger men. They had exchanged little more than greetings.

Aldric shook his head.

"We're not going to settle this by throwing names around."

"Then how do you suggest we settle it?"

Aldric looked towards the elder.

The elder rubbed his forehead.

"We have to find someone who can serve without leaving a household unable to manage."

"Everyone leaves someone behind," a woman said.

No one disagreed.

Eric looked down at the floor.

He had begun to understand the shape of the problem. It wasn't simply that nobody wanted to go. Each person considered had someone who would suffer from their absence.

And there was no way to make that suffering disappear.

***

Aldric spoke again.

"There's another possibility."

The room quieted.

He looked towards Eric.

"Vane."

Eric didn't recognise the name immediately. It took him a moment to realise Aldric was looking at him.

"Eric," Kael corrected.

Aldric's gaze shifted briefly.

"Eric, then."

Eric straightened.

Aldric continued. "He's young, healthy, and strong enough to work. He has no household of his own, no land to tend, and no family here depending on him."

The room became very still.

Eric felt the attention settle on him.

"He has been here only a few weeks," Aldric added. "Kael took him in, and the village has helped him. But if we're looking for someone who can leave without putting another household in difficulty, we have to consider him."

A farmer near the back frowned.

"He's been working harder than some of us."

"That isn't what I'm questioning."

"He helped us bring in the harvest," another villager said. "And he carried timber for the repairs. He's helped with the firewood."

"He helped my household too," someone else added. "We were behind with our work before he came."

Aldric looked around.

"I know he's helped. I'm not denying that."

"Then why are we speaking as if he's got nothing to lose?"

Aldric's jaw tightened.

"Because the rest of us have families who depend on us. Because some of our households cannot manage without their strongest workers. We have to consider the whole village."

His voice wasn't harsh, but there was little room for argument in it.

Kael stepped forward.

"Eric isn't a spare tool we can hand over because he has no land."

Aldric faced him.

"I didn't say he was."

"You said he could leave without putting anyone in difficulty."

"Is that not true?"

Kael took a moment before answering.

"He has worked here. He has helped the people in this room. We've shared our food and our homes with him. He's been part of our work these past weeks."

"I know."

"Then don't speak as though he has no place here."

Aldric looked at him for a long moment.

"Having a place here doesn't change what the order demands."

Kael's expression hardened.

"No. It doesn't."

The two men fell silent.

Lena stepped forward from behind them.

"He helped us," she said.

Her voice wasn't loud, but people turned towards her.

"He helped Father with the wood. He carried things for the farmers. He worked at the communal building. People have asked him for help because they know he'll do it."

She looked towards Aldric.

"Why are we talking about him as though he doesn't belong here?"

Aldric didn't answer immediately.

"Because belonging and being able to stay are not always the same thing," he said at last.

Lena looked unhappy with that answer, but she didn't speak again.

Eric remained still.

He hadn't expected to hear anyone defend him. He had known the villagers were becoming more comfortable with him, but he hadn't realised how many had noticed the work he had done.

It was a strange feeling.

A few weeks ago, he had arrived at the edge of this village with nothing. He had been unable to explain who he was or where he had come from. He could barely understand the people speaking around him.

Now they were arguing over whether he should be sent away.

Not because he was unwanted.

Because he was useful.

And because, in the eyes of some, he had less to lose.

***

The discussion continued, but Eric heard less of it.

He watched the people around him.

The farmer who had offered his eldest son stood beside his wife. The miller's relative had been mentioned twice, and each time someone had explained why the household needed him. Silas's mother was standing near the wall, her hands clasped tightly together.

There was no easy choice among them.

Eric thought about what would happen if he refused.

The village would continue arguing. They might find someone eventually, or the officials might choose. Someone would leave, and a household would lose a pair of hands it needed.

He looked towards Kael.

The older man had given him shelter without asking for anything in return. He had checked his injuries, fed him, and found work for him when he was ready. Lena had patiently helped him understand the language and had never made him feel foolish when he struggled.

The villagers had done much the same in smaller ways. They had allowed him to work beside them. They had shared what they had. They had begun to greet him as someone familiar.

He didn't want to leave.

That was the first thing he knew with certainty.

He had only just started to understand this place. He had only just found a routine that made his days feel normal again. There were still so many things he didn't know, and he had barely begun to learn about the world beyond Eastwood.

But staying meant allowing the village to carry the burden of deciding who else would go.

He thought about the alternatives.

The village couldn't afford the grain. He had heard enough to know that much. The people being considered all had someone depending on them, or work that would become harder without them.

He had no land. No family in Eastwood. No one who would be left without food because he was gone.

That didn't mean he had nothing to lose.

He had Kael and Lena now. He had people who would notice his absence.

But perhaps that was exactly why he should consider it.

There was something else, too.

He had spent fourteen years living in a world that wasn't his. He had returned to the place he once called home, only to discover that it was no longer the home he remembered. He didn't know whether his family was alive. He didn't know what had happened to them or where he might begin looking.

He couldn't spend the rest of his life waiting in a village simply because it was safe.

The army would take him beyond Eastwood. He would see other settlements, towns, perhaps cities. He might learn more about the Empire and the world he had returned to. He would have food and shelter, and he would earn money for his service.

It was not a pleasant prospect. He had no idea what military life would be like, and the stories he had heard were hardly reassuring.

Still, it was a way forward.

The villagers were still talking when Eric realised he had made up his mind.

He took a breath.

"I'll go."

The words were not loud.

But the room fell silent.

Kael turned towards him.

"Eric."

Eric looked at the elder.

"I'll be the one Eastwood sends."

For a moment, nobody answered.

The elder studied him.

"Do you understand what you're offering?"

"I think so."

"Military service isn't a short journey. You'll be expected to follow orders and serve wherever you're sent."

Eric nodded.

"I understand."

Kael stepped closer.

"You don't have to do this."

Eric looked at him.

"I know."

"Then don't decide because you think you owe us something."

"I'm not."

Kael held his gaze, searching his face.

Eric struggled to find the right words. He knew what he wanted to say, but the language still didn't always give him the words he needed.

"The village needs someone," he said. "The others have families. Work. People who depend on them."

"So do you," Lena said.

Eric turned towards her.

She was looking at him, her expression tense.

"I know."

"Then why?"

He hesitated.

"Because I can go."

It wasn't the whole answer, but it was the part he could say.

The elder looked towards Kael.

"He's offering himself."

Kael's jaw tightened.

"I heard him."

Aldric had said nothing. He stood with his arms folded, but his earlier certainty had faded. He looked at Eric now, not as an easy solution, but as a young man who had just volunteered to leave his home.

After a moment, Aldric lowered his eyes.

"If he's willing, the village can meet the order without taking someone from another household."

A farmer near the back shook his head.

"That doesn't make it right."

"No," Aldric said. "It doesn't."

The elder raised his voice.

"Then we will put his name forward. The officials must still accept him as the recruit, but this should satisfy the village's obligation."

He looked at Eric.

"Are you certain?"

Eric glanced around the room.

Some people looked relieved. Others avoided his eyes. The farmer whose son had been considered gave him a small, awkward nod. The miller's relative stood quietly beside his uncle.

Nobody seemed happy.

Perhaps that was the only honest reaction.

Eric nodded.

"I'm certain."

***

The meeting broke apart slowly.

People began leaving in small groups, speaking more quietly than they had when they arrived. The soldiers were informed of the village's intention, and their leader said he would record the name once the elder had completed the necessary paperwork.

The order had not yet been fulfilled. Eric would still have to be presented at Greybridge before the deadline.

But Eastwood had made its choice.

Aldric approached him as he stood near the doorway.

"You're making a difficult decision," he said.

Eric wasn't sure how to answer.

Aldric glanced towards the villagers leaving the building.

"I meant what I said earlier. We need every pair of hands we can get. You've helped more than I expected."

Eric nodded.

"I tried."

"I know."

Aldric hesitated.

"Some will be grateful. Some will feel guilty. Don't mistake either for how they truly think of you."

He gave Eric a brief nod and walked away.

Eric watched him go.

He still didn't know what to make of Aldric. The man could be difficult, but he had never been careless about the village. Perhaps that was why he could say things that hurt without seeming to realise how they sounded.

Kael was waiting outside.

Lena stood beside him.

Nobody spoke as they began walking home.

The afternoon was cold, and the wind moved through the bare branches along the path. Eric could hear the distant sounds of the village returning to its work. A cart creaked near the storehouse. Someone called to a child. A hammer struck wood somewhere beyond the houses.

Ordinary sounds.

The same sounds he had grown used to hearing.

Except now he knew he would be leaving them behind.

Lena walked a little ahead, her hands tucked into her sleeves. Kael kept pace beside Eric.

After a while, Kael spoke.

"We'll talk tonight."

Eric nodded.

There was still much to arrange. The journey, the supplies, the things he would need to take with him. Three days wasn't much time.

But for the first time since waking in this world, Eric knew where he was going.

He wasn't sure whether that made him feel better.

By the time they reached the house, the sun had begun to sink behind the trees.

Eastwood looked much as it always had. Small houses, narrow paths, smoke rising from chimneys. The wooden palisade stood around it, weathered and familiar.

A place he had entered with nothing.

A place he would soon leave with more than he had ever expected.

Eric paused at the door.

Then he followed Kael and Lena inside.
