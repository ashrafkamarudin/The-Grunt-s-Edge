Chapter 11: The First Day
The cold woke Eric before the morning bell.

He opened his eyes and stared at the wooden ceiling above him. For a few seconds, he didn't move. The blanket was warm enough, and the air outside it looked thoroughly unpleasant.

A muffled groan came from somewhere nearby.

Someone else was awake.

Eric turned his head. A few recruits were already getting out of their bunks, moving about quietly as they gathered their clothes and equipment.

He sighed and pushed the blanket aside.

The cold greeted him immediately.

Wonderful.

He sat up and rubbed his face. His body felt reasonably rested, though his feet were still tender from the journey. He pulled back his sock and checked the bandage. It was clean, and the skin underneath hadn't worsened.

His knee was a little stiff too.

Nothing serious, he hoped.

He dressed carefully, making sure his socks sat comfortably against his feet. Then he gathered his cloak and stepped down from the bunk.

Across the barracks, Derrin was already dressed and tightening the straps on his boots.

"Morning," Eric greeted him.

"Morning! Looks like winter decided to join our training."

Derrin glanced towards the window, where a thin layer of snow covered the ground outside. It had continued falling through the night, leaving the rooftops, fences and open yard dusted in white.

"A little more snow than yesterday."

"Could be worse. At least it isn't raining."

Tomas, who was checking his own equipment nearby, gave them a brief look.

"Give it time."

"You really know how to encourage people, Tomas."

Derrin laughed.

Eric chuckled and finished getting ready.

They left the barracks with the other recruits.

The snow crunched softly beneath their boots. It wasn't deep, and patches of earth were still visible along the paths, but the yard looked quite different from the previous evening.

The air was cold enough to sting his cheeks. Every breath left a faint cloud in front of him.

Winter's really starting.

The recruits gathered at the training yard.

A sergeant stood near the centre, watching as everyone found their places. His eyes moved over the formation, occasionally stopping when someone was standing out of line or struggling with their equipment.

"Straighten up. Keep your spacing."

The recruits adjusted themselves.

"You're not here to admire the weather. Eyes forward."

The last few conversations died away.

Once everyone had assembled, the sergeant divided them into smaller groups. Different instructors would take charge of each group, and the recruits would rotate through a series of exercises.

Eric found himself assigned to a group near the far side of the yard.

Derrin and Tomas were placed together in another group.

Eric noticed them moving towards their instructor. Derrin glanced back briefly, caught Eric looking, and raised his hand in a small greeting.

Eric returned it.

Then their instructors began giving orders.

There was little time for anything else.

Their first exercise was running.

The instructor, a broad-shouldered soldier with a weathered face, stood in front of them.

"Several laps around the yard. Keep your pace steady. Don't sprint at the beginning and collapse halfway through."

He looked over the group.

"If you fall behind, catch up. If you need to slow down, slow down. But don't stop unless I tell you."

He raised his hand.

"Move!"

The recruits started running.

Eric settled into a comfortable pace near the middle of the group.

The first lap was manageable. His legs warmed up, and his breathing remained steady. He had done plenty of physical work in Eastwood. Carrying supplies, gathering wood and helping with repairs had kept him active.

But running was different.

There was no load to carry, no particular destination and no task waiting at the end. Just the same stretch of ground, over and over again.

By the second lap, he was beginning to understand the problem.

By the third, his breathing had grown heavier.

Huff... haa... haa...

His boots landed against the uneven ground. The snow had been trampled into patches of mud, while other areas remained slippery.

He watched his footing.

Don't rush. Keep the pace.

A recruit ahead of him began slowing down. Another had already fallen several steps behind.

Eric could feel his own legs getting heavier.

Just keep going.

The instructor watched from the edge of the yard.

"Don't bunch up! Keep your distance!"

Eric adjusted his pace.

A little farther.

Then a little more.

When the instructor finally called them to stop, several recruits immediately bent forward, hands resting on their knees.

Eric did the same for a moment before remembering the instruction.

"Walk it off! Don't sit down!"

He straightened and began walking slowly around the yard.

His lungs were working hard.

That was only the beginning?

He glanced towards the other groups.

Derrin and Tomas were running along the opposite side of the yard. Both were keeping a steady pace, moving comfortably with the rest of their group.

Derrin seemed to have plenty of energy left. Even while running, he occasionally called something to the recruit beside him, earning a tired laugh in return.

Then he turned his head.

Their eyes met.

Despite the exercise, Derrin flashed him a broad grin and raised a thumb.

Eric couldn't help smiling.

Still cheerful, huh?

He let out a quiet chuckle before returning his attention to his own group.

The next exercises focused on strength and conditioning.

The instructor demonstrated a series of movements before ordering them to follow.

Squats.

Push-ups.

More squats.

Eric lowered himself for another push-up.

His arms trembled slightly as he pushed back up.

Come on...

He managed another.

Then another.

By the next repetition, his arms were beginning to complain.

He paused briefly at the top, breathing through his mouth.

"Keep going."

Eric glanced up.

The instructor was watching him.

He lowered himself again.

Right. Of course.

Around him, the other recruits were having their own difficulties. Some were stronger, moving through the exercises with little trouble. Others were struggling to maintain their form.

One young man seemed to be doing everything he could to avoid the full movement. He barely lowered himself before pushing back up.

"All the way down," the instructor said.

The recruit hesitated.

"Did you not hear me?"

The recruit quickly corrected himself.

Eric looked away and continued.

It was tempting to take the easy way out. His arms were already tired, and nobody would probably notice if he shortened a few repetitions.

But the instructor was watching.

More importantly, he knew he would only be cheating himself.

If I'm going to do this, I might as well do it properly.

He finished the exercise, then stood with the others.

His legs and arms were already feeling the strain.

They moved on to marching drills.

The instructor arranged them into lines and demonstrated how to maintain their spacing.

"Watch the man in front of you. Keep your pace. Don't wander off because you're tired or distracted."

The group began moving.

At first, Eric thought it would be easy.

It wasn't.

Some recruits stepped too quickly. Others fell behind. Keeping everyone in step required more attention than he expected.

They were stopped several times.

"Again."

They returned to the starting point.

"Again."

Eric adjusted his position and tried once more.

It was repetitive, but he could see the purpose. They weren't simply learning to walk in a line. They were learning to move together, to pay attention and to follow instructions without constantly needing someone to correct them.

By the time the exercise ended, Eric was ready for a break.

Unfortunately, the break was short.

The morning continued with more running and basic conditioning.

The snow had become patchy across the yard, with muddy footprints covering much of the ground. The occasional snowflake still drifted down, settling briefly on the recruits' cloaks before melting.

Eric was beginning to feel the cold again whenever they stopped moving.

His fingers were stiff, and his legs ached.

How many more exercises are there?

He glanced at the instructor.

The soldier was talking to another recruit.

Probably a lot.

When the next exercise began, Eric followed the instructions as best he could.

He wasn't particularly fast, but he wasn't the slowest either. He found himself somewhere in the middle, surrounded by recruits with different strengths and weaknesses.

One was clearly stronger than him.

Another had better endurance.

A third seemed to struggle with coordination, frequently stumbling whenever they had to change direction.

Eric began noticing these differences.

Being physically capable wasn't just about strength.

Some people could lift heavier loads but tired quickly. Others weren't particularly strong but could keep moving for much longer.

He wondered where he stood among them.

He didn't have much time to think about it.

"Move!"

He ran again.

Huff... haa... haa...

By midday, the recruits were finally dismissed for a meal.

Eric walked towards the mess hall with the other members of his group. His legs felt heavier than they had that morning, and his stomach was beginning to demand attention.

He collected his food and found a place to sit.

The meal was simple, but he ate more than usual.

He glanced around the hall and spotted Derrin and Tomas at another table.

Derrin was talking to a couple of recruits from his group. Even from a distance, Eric could see him smiling and gesturing with one hand.

Tomas sat nearby, eating quietly.

They seemed to be doing well enough.

Eric returned his attention to his own meal.

There wasn't much reason to interrupt them. They had their own group, and he had his.

Besides, he was too hungry to spend much time talking.

After finishing, he returned his bowl and stepped outside.

The snow was still falling lightly.

He looked towards the training yard.

The morning's exercises had left the ground in a sorry state. What had once been a thin white covering was now a mixture of mud, slush and trampled snow.

A soldier was clearing one of the paths with a shovel.

Eric pulled his cloak tighter.

And we're only halfway through the day.

The afternoon began with more conditioning.

Eric's body had cooled during the meal break, and his muscles felt stiff when he started moving again.

Their instructor seemed completely unconcerned.

"Get into position."

The recruits gathered.

They were told to run short distances, change direction when instructed, and repeat a series of movements.

Nothing particularly complicated.

But it was harder than it looked.

His legs were tired, and the uneven ground made it difficult to maintain his footing. Each time he changed direction, he had to be careful not to slip.

He glanced towards the other groups.

Derrin and Tomas were doing a similar exercise nearby.

Tomas moved steadily, following the instructions without much fuss. Derrin was keeping pace beside the others, his movements confident and controlled.

He was clearly tired too. Sweat had gathered along his forehead, and his breathing was heavier than it had been that morning.

But he didn't slow down.

At one point, a recruit from his group began falling behind. The young man stumbled slightly while changing direction, then struggled to regain his pace.

Derrin noticed.

He moved alongside him, keeping his own pace as he spoke.

"Come on! Keep moving. We've got this!"

The recruit shook his head, breathing hard.

"Don't tell me you're giving up already. I haven't even finished suffering yet!"

Derrin laughed.

The recruit managed a tired smile and started moving again.

Derrin stayed beside him for a little longer before returning to his position.

Eric watched for a moment.

He really does pay attention to everyone around him.

Derrin looked across the yard and caught Eric watching.

He flashed another grin.

Eric smiled back.

Then his instructor's voice snapped him out of it.

"Eyes forward!"

Eric quickly faced the front.

"Unless you want to run another round?"

"No, sir."

"Then pay attention."

Noted.

They continued.

As the afternoon wore on, the recruits began to show signs of fatigue.

Some slowed down whenever the instructor looked away. Others started cutting corners, doing fewer repetitions or taking longer breaks than they were supposed to.

Their instructor noticed.

He let it continue for a little while.

Then he stopped the group.

"What's wrong? Tired already?"

Nobody answered.

He looked across the recruits.

"I've seen some of you slowing down. Others barely putting in any effort. You think we're doing this for fun?"

A few recruits lowered their eyes.

The soldier stepped closer.

"Train like your life depends on it! Because someday, it may really be. And that day may come sooner than you think!"

His voice carried across the yard.

For a moment, the recruits stood quietly.

The instructor looked at them one by one.

"You think the things you learn here are just exercises? You think it matters only while you're standing in this yard?"

He shook his head.

"Out there, nobody is going to wait for you to catch your breath. Nobody is going to stop because your legs hurt. If you can't keep up, you become a burden to the people beside you. And if you can't do what you're supposed to do, you may not get another chance."

He pointed towards the starting line.

"Now move!"

The recruits hurried back into position.

Eric followed.

His breathing was already heavy.

Huff... haa... haa...

The instructor's words lingered in his mind.

Train like your life depends on it.

He remembered the things he had heard the previous evening.

Tracks near the southern farms.

A patrol recalled from the eastern farms.

Something moving closer to the road.

At the time, they had sounded like ordinary conversations between soldiers. Things that happened somewhere beyond the training station.

But they weren't ordinary.

There were monsters out there.

And the soldiers knew it.

Eric continued running.

His legs were beginning to ache, and his breathing was becoming ragged.

Just keep moving.

He wasn't the fastest in the group. He wasn't the strongest either.

But he could still move.

So he did.

The afternoon training finally ended.

Eric returned to the barracks with the other recruits, feeling as though every part of his body had been worked over.

The snow had accumulated a little more around the buildings. It gathered along the fence and the edges of the paths, while the main walkways had been trampled into muddy slush.

He removed his outer clothing and checked his feet.

The bandage was still in place. The skin was tender, but it hadn't worsened.

His knee was stiff, though there was no swelling.

He remembered the medical staff's warning not to push too hard during the first few days.

Can't afford to injure myself now.

He cleaned himself as best he could, changed into dry clothing and hung his damp socks where they could dry.

The soldiers had reminded them repeatedly to keep their equipment in order.

He was beginning to understand why.

Wet clothing, damp bedding and neglected equipment would become serious problems once winter grew harsher.

After the evening meal, Eric returned to the barracks.

Some recruits were already lying on their bunks. Others were talking quietly or tending to their equipment.

Across the room, Derrin was still talking with a few recruits from his group.

He was tired, that much was obvious. His shoulders slumped occasionally, and he rubbed one arm while listening to another recruit.

Yet he was smiling.

At one point, he said something that made the others laugh.

Eric watched for a moment before turning away.

Some people really don't seem to run out of energy.

He sat on his bunk and slowly stretched his legs.

His shoulders ached. His arms felt weak, and his thighs were sore.

He leaned back against the wall and closed his eyes.

Haaah...

He let out a long breath.

One day.

That was all it had taken to make him feel this exhausted.

And this was only the beginning.

The instructor's words returned to him.

Train like your life depends on it.

Eric opened his eyes.

He thought about why he had come to Grayford.

He hadn't volunteered because he wanted to become a soldier. He hadn't come looking for glory, recognition or some grand adventure.

He had volunteered because someone had to go.

And if he hadn't stepped forward, someone else might have been made to take his place.

He had made that choice himself.

Now he was here.

He thought about the people back in Eastwood. Kael. Lena. The village and the people who had remained there.

He had no idea what would happen beyond the training station. Perhaps the soldiers would keep the monsters away. Perhaps the situation would worsen.

He didn't know.

But he knew one thing.

He didn't want to die.

Not after making that decision. Not after leaving everyone behind.

He wanted to return.

And if he was going to have any chance of doing that, he needed to prepare himself.

Today's training had made one thing painfully clear.

He wasn't particularly strong. His endurance wasn't exceptional, and he had no idea how he would fare in an actual fight.

The scheduled training would teach him the basics, but he didn't think he should rely on that alone.

Perhaps he could do a little extra running in the morning.

Some simple exercises to build his strength. Work on his balance and stamina.

Nothing too strenuous. Not while his feet and knee were still recovering.

He would have to be careful.

But he could start small.

I need to get stronger.

He looked towards the window.

Snow continued falling outside, slowly covering the ground.

The cold was only going to get worse.

And whatever dangers waited beyond Grayford weren't going to care whether he was tired, sore or ready.

Eric pulled his blanket over himself.

Tomorrow, he would train again.

And when the scheduled training was over, he would find a little more time for himself.

He wasn't going to leave his survival entirely in someone else's hands.

He had chosen to come here.

Now he had to make sure he could see that choice through.

He closed his eyes.

For tonight, sleep would have to come first.
