# Chapter 14: Two Different Paths

The days following the magical assessment passed quietly.

Training resumed as usual, and the recruits gradually returned to their familiar routines. The excitement surrounding the test had mostly faded, though conversations about magical aptitude still surfaced from time to time. Those who had received promising results were beginning to receive instructions about their next steps, while everyone else returned to their regular duties.

Derrin was among those who had been selected.

He had been informed that his results qualified him for specialized mage training. It was an opportunity few commoners ever received, and he had spent the past few days talking about little else.

Not that anyone could blame him.

Even Eric and Tomas had grown accustomed to hearing about it.

"Do you think they'll teach him to shoot fires?" Tomas asked one morning as they prepared for training.

"Probably," Eric replied.

"Then we'll have to be careful around him."

"Why?"

"Because he'll probably set something on fire before he learns how to control it."

Eric chuckled. "You should tell him that."

As if summoned by their conversation, Derrin appeared behind them.

"Did someone mention fireballs?"

Tomas closed his eyes briefly.

"Unfortunately."

Derrin grinned and placed his hands on his hips. "You two should start showing me some respect. Soon, I'll be a proper mage. You might even have to address me as Master Derrin."

"Let's see if you can survive the training first," Tomas replied.

"Ha! Have some faith in me!"

Eric smiled at their exchange. It was reassuring to see that Derrin hadn't changed much despite receiving such an opportunity. He was still the same loud, energetic recruit who had somehow managed to turn even ordinary training days into something entertaining.

However, that morning, Derrin wasn't there just to boast.

He had received further instructions.

A soldier had informed him that his departure for specialized training had been arranged. He was expected to pack his belongings and report to the designated gathering point later that day.

It was finally time to leave.

***

The news spread quickly through the barracks.

Some recruits congratulated Derrin, while others gathered around to ask about his new training arrangement. A few were curious about where he would be sent, and whether he would still be allowed to visit.

Derrin, naturally, answered every question with more confidence than knowledge.

"I'll be learning all sorts of things," he declared while stuffing his belongings into his bag. "Mana control, spells, maybe even some advanced techniques!"

"You haven't even started yet," Tomas pointed out.

"Which is why I have to start somewhere!"

"Try not to get yourself injured."

"Don't worry. I'll be fine."

Eric watched from his bunk, smiling as Derrin continued packing. It was strange seeing him prepare to leave. They hadn't known each other for very long, but the three of them had spent enough time together that his departure felt more significant than Eric had expected.

Derrin eventually finished packing and slung his bag over his shoulder.

"That's everything!" he announced.

Tomas glanced at his bag. "You sure? Wouldn't want you showing up at mage training without your underwear."

"Please. I have everything under control."

"That's what worries me."

Eric laughed.

Derrin grinned, but then his expression softened a little as he looked at the two of them.

"Well... I guess this is goodbye for now."

"Looks like it," Eric replied.

Derrin scratched the back of his head.

"Honestly, it's a little strange leaving you two behind. We only just started training together."

Tomas shrugged. "It's not like you're going to another kingdom."

"Still! Who knows when we'll see each other again?"

"We'll manage," Tomas said. "Just don't forget about us when you become some big-shot mage."

Derrin looked offended.

"Forget you two? Never!"

He raised his chin, putting on his most confident expression.

"And if either of you ever gets into trouble, just use my name! The great Mage Derrin! Heh!"

Tomas stared at him.

"Wouldn't that make things worse?"

"Hey! Have some faith in me!"

Eric chuckled. "We'll keep that in mind."

A soldier waiting outside called out to Derrin.

"Well, that's my cue!" Derrin adjusted his bag and turned towards the entrance. Then he looked back at them, flashing his familiar grin.

"I won't forget you two! So don't go getting yourselves killed before I come back!"

"Just go already!" Tomas called.

"I'll see you again, Derrin. Good luck!"

Derrin waved enthusiastically.

"I'll be seeing you around, folks! The great Derrin leaves!"

And with that, he followed the waiting soldier out of the barracks.

Eric and Tomas watched him disappear beyond the entrance.

For a while, neither said anything.

Then Tomas glanced at the empty space beside them.

"Well, it's going to be quieter now."

"Definitely," Eric replied.

***

Life continued.

The next morning, the recruits were awakened by the familiar signal echoing through the barracks. They gathered, washed, dressed and prepared for the day's training.

There was no Derrin joking around while struggling to put on his equipment, nor was there anyone making ridiculous claims about becoming the greatest mage in the kingdom.

Eric and Tomas went about their morning quietly.

They ate together, assembled with the others and followed the instructions given by their sergeant. Their conversations were shorter than before, usually limited to practical things or an occasional remark about training.

It wasn't that they were unhappy.

They simply had less to talk about.

As the days passed, Eric found himself thinking more often about his own circumstances.

Derrin had been given a direction to pursue. He would be learning magic, developing his abilities and eventually finding his place among the kingdom's mages.

Eric, on the other hand, was still a recruit with no clear idea of what came next.

One evening, after a particularly exhausting day of training, Eric lay on his bunk and stared at the wooden ceiling above him.

His body ached, but he was too tired to do much besides rest.

He began thinking about his future.

More specifically, he started wondering what he actually wanted to do.

*Should I try to return to Earth?*

The question had crossed his mind several times since arriving in this world, but he had never seriously considered what returning would involve.

For one thing, this was supposed to be his original world.

If that was true, could he even return to Earth? And if he could, how?

He had no idea how he had ended up there in the first place. There was no obvious explanation for what had happened, and certainly no clear way to reverse it.

*Maybe magic could help.*

His thoughts briefly wandered towards the magical assessment.

The crystal, the mages conducting the test, the explanations about magical cores and mana...

But he had no magical core.

Whatever possibilities magic might have offered him, they suddenly seemed much further away.

*Well, that's unfortunate.*

He turned onto his side.

*Then what should I do here?*

He was in the army now. He had food, shelter and a place to train. The military provided everything he needed to get by, at least for the time being.

But what happened after?

Would he become a full-time soldier?

He had heard that military service paid reasonably well compared to other occupations, particularly for commoners. He wasn't entirely sure how the payment system worked, though. He still hadn't properly familiarized himself with the local currency.

*Maybe I could stay in the army. The pay is apparently pretty good.*

He considered it for a moment.

*Or I could leave after my training and return to Eastwood. Find somewhere to live and get a normal job.*

That didn't sound too bad either.

Then another thought occurred to him.

*What about my family?*

Eric's expression changed slightly.

He had been so occupied with adjusting to this world that he hadn't spent much time thinking about them.

Were they still alive?

Where were they now?

Did they even know what had happened to him?

He didn't know enough about his circumstances to answer any of those questions. If he wanted to find them, he would first need to understand where he came from and what had happened before he ended up in the fortress.

*Maybe I should look for them.*

He stared at the ceiling again.

There were quite a few things he needed to figure out. Returning to Earth, finding his family, deciding whether to remain in the army or make a life elsewhere.

He had no clear answers.

And yet, despite all those important matters, his thoughts kept drifting towards the same subject.

*Magic...*

Eric frowned.

*No. Focus.*

He returned to thinking about his future.

*If I stay in the army, I could earn money, gain experience and eventually become a proper soldier. That might not be so bad. I could travel, visit other places and perhaps learn more about this world.*

*Magic...*

His eyebrow twitched.

*Seriously?*

He tried to ignore it.

*Maybe I could return to Eastwood eventually. It seems like a decent place. I could find work, save some money and perhaps start looking for my family.*

*Magic...*

Eric pulled his blanket over his head.

*I get it!*

He lay still for a few seconds.

Then he rolled onto his back again.

*But still...*

Magic.

He remembered the announcement that had sent the recruits into a frenzy. The magical assessment. Derrin's result. The excitement on his friend's face when he discovered that he had a magical core.

He remembered watching the crystal glow during the assessment and wondering what it would feel like to perform something like that himself.

He wanted to know how mana worked. How spells were formed. How mages controlled their abilities.

There were so many things he wanted to understand.

And he couldn't do any of them.

"Arghhhh! I want to learn magic, god damn it!"

Several recruits turned towards him.

Eric froze.

A brief silence followed.

"...Sorry."

One of the recruits shook his head and returned to his own business. Another muttered something before rolling over in bed.

Eric slowly pulled his blanket over his face.

*This is ridiculous.*

He remained like that for a while before another thought crept into his mind.

*Wait.*

He lowered the blanket.

*Derrin.*

His friend was going to become a mage. He would be learning all sorts of things about magic, and eventually he would have actual experience using it.

*Couldn't I just ask him?*

Eric sat up slightly.

*Once he's learned enough, maybe he could teach me something.*

He knew he couldn't simply become a mage. The assessment had been clear about his lack of a magical core. But that didn't necessarily mean he couldn't learn anything about magic at all.

There was still so much he didn't know.

Could he learn about mana without having a core? Could he study the principles behind spells? Was there any way for an ordinary person to interact with magic?

He had no answers.

But Derrin might know more in the future.

*And if I want to catch up with him, I'll need to get stronger too.*

Eric's expression gradually brightened.

*That's it!*

He lay back down, looking rather pleased with himself.

*Just you wait, Derrin. When we meet again, you're teaching me magic!*

He grinned at the ceiling.

Of course, there was no guarantee that Derrin would be able to teach him anything. There was also no guarantee that Eric could ever use magic, regardless of how much he learned.

But for now, he had found something worth pursuing.

He wanted to become stronger, learn more about this world and, eventually, find someone who could teach him about magic.

And if that someone happened to be his old friend, all the better.

***

The following morning, Eric woke with a slightly different attitude.

The familiar signal sounded, and the recruits began preparing for the day. Eric dressed, checked his equipment and joined the others outside.

The winter air was sharp against his face. Snow covered parts of the training grounds, while soldiers moved between buildings carrying supplies and equipment.

"Fall in!"

The sergeant's voice rang across the yard.

The recruits quickly formed their lines.

"Straighten your formation! Close the gaps! Eyes forward!"

Eric adjusted his position.

"Better. Listen carefully. Today we'll be continuing your basic conditioning before moving on to weapons practice. I expect everyone to follow instructions and maintain discipline. Understood?"

"Yes, Sergeant!"

"Good. Move!"

The recruits began their exercises.

Eric focused on keeping his pace steady. His body was still sore from previous sessions, but he tried not to let that distract him. He paid closer attention to his movements and corrected his posture whenever he noticed himself slipping.

Tomas was running nearby.

"You're unusually quiet today," Tomas remarked.

"Am I?"

"More than usual."

Eric gave him a small smile. "Just thinking."

"About what?"

"Getting stronger."

Tomas looked at him briefly.

"That's a change."

Eric chuckled. "What's that supposed to mean?"

"Nothing."

They continued running.

After the conditioning exercises, the recruits were moved to a separate section of the training grounds for basic weapons instruction.

Wooden practice weapons had been arranged in several rows. Each recruit was assigned one, and the instructor began demonstrating the proper way to hold it.

Eric listened carefully.

He learned how to position his feet, maintain his balance and keep his grip steady. The movements seemed simple when demonstrated, but performing them correctly required more effort than he expected.

"Don't swing with your entire body," the instructor corrected one recruit. "Control your movements. If you lose your balance every time you attack, your opponent won't need to do much to defeat you."

Eric adjusted his stance.

He wasn't particularly good at it yet, but he made an effort to remember each correction.

For the first time, he found himself thinking less about simply finishing the exercise and more about understanding how to do it properly.

Tomas, standing beside him, was having similar difficulties.

"Your grip is too loose," Eric pointed out.

Tomas looked at his hand.

"So it is."

"Try holding it like this."

Tomas adjusted his grip.

"Better?"

"Looks better."

"Then let's see if it actually works."

They returned to their practice.

It wasn't much, but it was a start.

***

A few days later, the recruits received another lesson.

This time, it wasn't about physical conditioning or weapon handling.

They were gathered near one of the fortress's larger training structures, where an experienced soldier had been assigned to explain some of the dangers found in the northern territories.

The recruits stood in several rows while the soldier addressed them.

"Since you're stationed in the North, you should understand what lives beyond these walls. Some of you already know. Others may have only heard stories. Either way, I expect you to pay attention."

He gestured towards a board behind him.

A rough illustration of a wolf-like creature had been drawn across it.

"Frostfang Wolves."

Several recruits exchanged glances.

"They're among the more common predators found in these regions. Their bodies are covered in thick fur, and they're well adapted to the cold. They usually hunt in packs, using their speed and numbers to overwhelm their prey."

The soldier pointed towards the illustration.

"Do not mistake their size for weakness. A single wolf can be dangerous to an untrained person. A pack is considerably worse."

One of the recruits raised his hand.

"Sergeant, how large are their packs usually?"

"It varies. You might encounter three or four. Larger packs have been reported, particularly in areas where prey is plentiful."

A recruit standing near the back spoke up.

"My village had trouble with them last winter. They kept coming after the livestock."

The soldier turned towards him.

"Where was your village?"

"Further north, near the wooded hills."

"Did anyone get hurt?"

The recruit hesitated.

"One of the hunters. He went out with two others to track them, but they were caught when the pack circled around them. He survived, though he lost part of his leg."

A few recruits went quiet.

The soldier nodded.

"That's why you don't approach them carelessly. Wolves are not stupid animals. They learn from their encounters with people, and a pack that has become accustomed to hunting near settlements can be particularly troublesome."

He moved to the next illustration.

This one depicted a much larger creature with a thick, hulking body and long arms.

"Snow Trolls."

A few recruits leaned forward.

"Unlike Frostfang Wolves, these creatures are not usually encountered in large groups. They're much larger, stronger and considerably more difficult to deal with."

The soldier tapped the drawing.

"Their bodies are exceptionally durable. Ordinary weapons may not be enough to bring one down quickly, and getting close to them is dangerous. If you encounter one, you do not attempt to be a hero. You report its location and wait for experienced personnel."

A recruit raised his hand.

"Have any of them attacked settlements?"

"Yes. There have been several incidents over the years."

Another recruit spoke up.

"My uncle was part of a hunting party that encountered one near our village. They said it tore through their equipment like it was nothing."

"Did they kill it?"

"No. They ran."

The soldier gave a brief nod.

"That was the correct decision."

A few recruits looked surprised.

"Remember this. Your job is not to prove how brave you are. Your job is to follow orders, protect your comrades and survive. There are creatures in these territories that you are not equipped to fight. Knowing when to retreat is just as important as knowing when to attack."

Eric listened carefully.

Until now, monsters had mostly been something he had heard about in passing. He knew they existed, and he had understood that the military was stationed in the North for a reason.

But hearing these accounts from people who had actually encountered them made the danger feel considerably more real.

These weren't creatures from stories or distant legends.

They were part of the world people lived in.

And ordinary villagers had to deal with them.

Eric glanced at the illustrations again.

Frostfang Wolves. Snow Trolls.

He committed the names to memory.

"Are there other monsters?" someone asked.

"Plenty," the soldier answered. "But these are two of the more relevant threats you should know about for now. You'll learn more as your training progresses."

He paused before continuing.

"Also, don't assume that every monster behaves the same way. Learn what you're dealing with before making a decision. A mistake in the field can cost you your life, or the lives of the people standing beside you."

The recruits remained silent.

The soldier seemed satisfied and continued explaining their general behaviour, habitats and the precautions soldiers should take when operating in areas where these creatures had been sighted.

Eric paid close attention.

He still had plenty of questions, but he understood that this was only an introduction. There would be time to learn more later.

***

A week passed.

The routine at the Winter Fortress gradually became familiar.

Morning signals, formations, physical conditioning, weapons practice and evening duties filled the recruits' days. The cold remained relentless, and fresh snow continued to gather along the fortress walls.

Eric and Tomas had settled into their new rhythm.

They spoke less than they used to when Derrin was around, but they had grown more accustomed to training together. Occasionally, one would encourage the other to keep going when an exercise became particularly exhausting.

Their efforts were beginning to show, though neither had made any remarkable leap in ability. Eric's endurance had improved slightly, and he was becoming more comfortable with the basic exercises and weapon handling.

There was still a long way to go.

He also found himself paying more attention to the things happening around the fortress. Reports from patrols, conversations between soldiers and the occasional mention of monster sightings had become things he listened for rather than ignored.

The North was not a particularly forgiving place, especially during winter.

As for Derrin, Eric hadn't heard much since his departure. He wondered how the training was going and whether his friend had already managed to perform his first spell.

He hoped so.

After all, he had someone to catch up with.

And someone who owed him a few magic lessons.

Eric smiled to himself at the thought before returning his attention to the day's training.

There was still plenty of work to do.
