The creatures keep coming.

At first it looks impossible that the line could hold against so many different things at once. Forest hounds are mixed among creatures I haven't seen before, some low and lean, others broad enough that they look like moving boulders from a distance, all of them spilling out of the trees in numbers that made my stomach tighten every time another wave appeared.

But somehow we hold.

The impacts come steadily now, rattling through the shield wall and into my arms, but not with the overwhelming force I remember from the hound assault. The pressure is spread across the formation instead of concentrated in one place. One section takes a hit, another recovers, then somewhere farther down the line shouting erupts before settling again.

The wall bends.

The wall flexes.

But it doesn't break.

My entire world shrinks to the shield in front of me and the men beside me. I can't see much beyond that. Every now and then I catch glimpses through gaps in the formation, flashes of claws, fur, teeth, or something stranger, but never long enough to properly understand what I was looking at.

Most of the killing happens behind us.

For a while I just hold, not thinking about what's happening around me. Sometimes a creature screams. Sometimes a man does. But I don't falter — I keep holding, because if I stop I know I won't make it.

Then, just as quickly, the pressure loosens for a few breaths, a strange, brief emptiness where nothing seems to be pressing against us. A moment of rest before the next wave arrives to replace it. It is ugly. Exhausting in a way that didn’t feel dramatic or meaningful, just constant strain through the arms, the back, the jaw clenched too long without noticing.

But it's working and I find myself breathing a little easier without meaning to. 

But as my confidence was building, I feel it.. the ground shifts under my boots, a heavier vibration than before, not the constant churn of bodies, but something deeper, like weight being dragged through the earth itself. I feel it travel up my legs and settle behind my ribs. Around me, men start looking up and outward instead of forward. Not at the hounds already pressing the wall but past them.

I shift slightly, trying to see through a narrow gap between shields ahead. For a moment, there is nothing that made sense. Then something moves beyond the horde.

Above them. Higher. Too large to belong to anything that had been part of this battle so far.

At first my mind refuses it. Just… refused but I can see it clearly. A shape, broad and uneven, pushing through the forest line like the trees themselves had to make space for it. The monsters around it didn’t attack. They move aside. Not organized, not respectful, just instinctive avoidance.

“What… is that?” I heard myself say.

“Trolls!” someone shouts.

The word doesn’t mean much to me. Just something heavy in tone, like it should mean something worse than it sounds. Darrin’s grip tightens on his spear shaft. I notice his knuckles first, white under dirt and dried blood, before I follow his stare fully.

The trolls don’t come straight at us. They move through the center of the formation instead, and for a brief moment it almost feels like luck, like maybe they’ve chosen the wrong direction and we’ve been spared the worst of it.

Then the feeling breaks.

“Ready your spears!” Corbin’s voice cuts through, sharp enough to drag me back into my body.

A second later, closer to shouting than command now.

“We can’t hold the line… there’s too many now. Kill them!”

I hesitate.

Just for a moment.

Spears? My mind catches on it like it doesn’t want to accept the change. Should I abandon the shield? It still feels like the only thing between me and everything out there, solid, familiar, something I understand in a way I don’t understand anything else in this place.

But I don’t drop it.

I keep it up.

I shift awkwardly instead, shield still locked in my left arm, spear in my right hand, the grip feeling wrong because I’ve never had to trust two things at once like this before.

Then the next wave reaches us.

There’s no space to think anymore.

I push the shield forward out of habit, letting it catch the first impact, but at the same time I stab with the spear, not aiming properly, not really seeing anything clearly through the gaps between bodies and metal, just reacting to movement, to pressure, to anything that looks like it’s about to reach me.

Steel bites into something soft.

Then something else hits my shield hard enough to jolt my shoulder.

I don’t even register what I hit or missed. I just keep going, pulling the spear back and thrusting again when something moves, like repetition is the only thing keeping me upright in the middle of it. Around me, the line isn’t clean anymore. It’s no longer a wall holding a shape, just people trying to survive the space in front of them however they can, shields half-used, spears stabbing through openings that appear and vanish too quickly to understand and some even ran.

It keeps going. I don't know for how long. I just raise my shield and stab, over and over — the only motion left in a body that's forgotten everything else. I can feel my stamina is bottoming out then suddenly an impact slams into my shield from the front. Then something from the side catches me at the same time, and my stance breaks before I even understand I’ve lost it and my knee hits the ground.

My shield is still up, but it’s wrong now. Tilted. Useless in the angle I’m holding it. Something moves into that space immediately. I try to bring the spear up— But my arm doesn’t answer fast enough. There’s a gap. Small, but enough. And I know, very clearly, something is about to fill it.

Then someone steps in front of me. Darrin.

No hesitation. No glance back at me. Just his body shifting into the space I failed to hold, shield coming up clean and tight like it belongs there more than mine ever did.

The impact hits him instead. His stance locking in as he absorbs the force and turns it just enough to stop it from breaking through. His spear moves at the same time. Not wild like mine. Controlled. Direct. It drives into whatever was pushing forward, and for a brief second I hear it—a wet, sharp break in motion, then the pressure shifts away from us.

“Get back up, Eric!”

His voice cuts through it, not loud, but close. Real.

Then harder, sharper.

“Up. Now!”

I waste no time and get back on my feet, forcing myself upright even before my body agrees with it. Everything aches in a way that feels delayed, like the pain is arriving a second too late to matter, but it still tries to claim space in my ribs, my shoulder, my legs.

I ignore it.

My arms scream and my legs feel heavy in a way that doesn’t make sense after only a short drop.

I ignore that too.

“You gotta stand and fight to survive, Eric!”

Darrin’s voice cuts through somewhere close, still holding his position in front of me, still covering the space I lost for even a moment.

“I know…” I answer, breath catching slightly as I force my grip tighter. “I know that… and I will.”

The words don’t feel strong. They don’t need to. They just have to exist.

The fight keeps going around us, but it’s different now. Less clean. Less coordinated. More like scattered collisions than a line holding together. I don’t have time to understand the shape of it anymore, only the pieces that reach me.

Shield up. Impact. Spear forward. Reset. 

I stay in that rhythm, moving through it like a machine that doesn’t need to think, only react until suddenly the spear meets nothing. No resistance. No push back. I blink, still half expecting impact, and hold the shield up anyway out of habit.

Another breath passes.

Nothing comes.

The world around me doesn’t really stop, it just… loosens, like something that’s been stretched too far finally letting go. I hear it before I see it—voices no longer shouting, just calling out in broken pieces, slower now, scattered, far away.

I lower the spear slightly.

Then I see it. The line is gone. Not broken in one place, but dissolved into uneven pockets of bodies and movement that no longer connect. Some men are still fighting in places, finishing what their arms started, but most of it has already ended in the way things end when no one is agreeing to continue.

A few monsters still move at the edge, but even they look different now, slower, pulling back toward the treeline in broken groups, like whatever was driving them forward has stopped.

"haa.. I.. I survived.." I gasp, my voice rough and barely there.

The battle is over. It doesn't feel like victory. Then it hits all at once. Relief, fatigue, pain, all of it stacking on top of each other until my legs just give out. I drop to my knees, and for a moment I don’t even try to catch myself. I just fall the rest of the way and end up lying there, staring up at the empty sky.

I can hear myself breathing too loud.

Ren and Darrin come into view above me a moment later. Their faces are covered in dirt and sweat, armor scratched, shoulders heavy like they’ve been carrying something far longer than just today. But there’s something in their eyes too—same thing I feel but don’t want to name properly.

Relief, maybe. Or just the fact that it’s over.

*“You alive, Eric?”* Ren asks.

“For now.” My voice comes out hoarse, like it belongs to someone else.

A pause.

“Did we all make it?”

“Hope so.”

Darrin lets out a short breath through his nose, almost a laugh.

“Good job not fainting this time,” he says, smirking a little despite everything.

I let out something that barely counts as a chuckle. “Yeah… well. I’m not done yet.”

Ren shakes his head slightly like that answer doesn’t matter much right now.

“Let’s go get some rest.”

He helps me up. My legs feel wrong at first, slow to remember what standing is supposed to be like, but they hold. We start walking back toward camp.

The camp looks… busy, but not alive in a normal way. Men tending wounds. Carrying the injured. Dragging bodies where they can. Some just sitting where they collapsed, staring at nothing like they’re waiting for their bodies to catch up.

The air is thick with smoke and iron. Blood. Sweat. Something else underneath it I can’t quite place. Not celebration. Not grief either. Just exhaustion so deep it doesn’t know what to do with itself yet.

I sit down near a stack of supply crates close to our tent and let my back fall against them. The pain is still there, but it’s not sharp anymore. More like something distant I don’t have to react to every second.

My breathing starts to slow without me forcing it.

Ren sits beside me, legs stretched out, eyes still scanning the camp out of habit more than need.

“You okay?” he asks again, quieter this time.

“I’m fine.” I answer.

It doesn’t sound convincing even to me.

He just nods anyway.

“Good.”

We don’t talk after that. The noise of the camp fades into the background, not stopping, just losing weight in my head until it doesn’t matter anymore. My eyes start to drift shut before I even realize it and I let them.

---

Corbin wakes us sometime in the late afternoon, when the light outside the camp has already started to shift into that dull, tired gold that means the day is running out. He doesn’t say much at first. Just stands there looking at the three of us like he’s already counted who is missing and decided not to comment on it.

“Mess is going to end soon. Better hurry if you want food.” he says finally and left.

It takes me a moment to process it. Food. My body reacts before my thoughts do, a slow shift from exhaustion into something closer to need. I sit up too fast and immediately regret it, my head pulling slightly behind my movement. Ren is already moving, pushing himself up with a grunt like he never fully sat down in the first place. Darrin follows a second later, slower, rolling his shoulder once like he’s checking what still works. I push myself up last. My legs feel heavy, not injured, just… used up. Like they’ve been asked to hold too much for too long and haven’t fully forgiven me yet.We start walking with Corbin without really deciding to.

The camp is still active, but in a different way now. Less urgency, more leftover motion. People moving because stopping completely feels worse than moving. Some are still carrying wounded toward the medical tents. Others sit in groups near fires that look more like habit than warmth. 

The mess hall is already crowded when we reach it. Long lines. Quiet voices. No one really talking above a normal tone anymore, like everyone forgot how loud they were supposed to be and we take our place in line. No one complains. No one pushes.

When the food finally comes, it’s the same simple ration as always. Thick, heavy, something between soup and porridge, enough to fill the stomach without asking for anything back. I eat without thinking much about it at first. Just swallowing, letting the warmth spread slowly through me in a way that feels almost unfamiliar after everything earlier. Ren sits across from me, eating slower than usual, eyes half on his bowl, half somewhere far away. Darrin eats like he’s just refueling something that never stopped working in the first place. Corbin sits a little off to the side, eating the same as the rest of us, saying nothing.

I finish before I realize I was even close to done. My body still feels like it’s waiting for impact that isn’t coming. Ren notices me staring into the empty bowl.

“Still there?” he asks.

I blink once.

“…yeah,” I say. “Just tired.”

He gives a small nod like that’s the only correct answer available. We don’t linger long after that. There’s nothing to linger for. We leave the mess hall with the rest of the camp’s slow exhale, and for the first time since the morning bell, no one is running.
