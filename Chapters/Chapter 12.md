Chapter 12: A Small Act of Courage
The days that followed began to settle into a routine.

Wake before sunrise. Wash, dress, and gather for formation. Train until the instructors were satisfied, or until they decided the recruits had suffered enough for one morning. Eat, rest briefly, then do it all over again.

Eric was beginning to recognize the rhythm.

He was also beginning to understand why the soldiers made everything look easier than it was.

The morning exercises had become slightly more familiar. He no longer struggled to keep track of every instruction, and his body was slowly adapting to the repeated running, marching, and conditioning.

Slowly.

His legs still ached by the end of each session, and his shoulders were rarely free of soreness. The cold didn't help either. Even when the sun climbed above the trees, the air remained sharp enough to make his fingers stiff.

At least the snow had stopped falling for now. A thin layer remained along the edges of the training yard, while the ground in the middle had turned into a mixture of mud and packed earth.

"Keep your pace! Don't slow down!"

The instructor's voice carried across the yard.

Eric took another breath.

Huff... haa...

His legs were getting heavy.

Around him, the other recruits continued running. Some looked just as exhausted as he felt. A few were managing better, while others were clearly struggling to keep up.

He focused on the back of the recruit ahead of him.

One step at a time.

He didn't need to be the fastest. He just needed to keep moving.

By the time the exercise ended, Eric's breathing was rough and his shirt clung uncomfortably to his back.

He bent forward, resting his hands on his knees.

"Still alive?"

Eric looked up.

Derrin stood nearby, looking tired but wearing his usual grin. Tomas was beside him, breathing steadily as he adjusted his sleeves.

"Somehow," Eric replied.

"Good. Wouldn't want you dropping dead before lunch."

"That's reassuring."

Derrin laughed.

Tomas glanced at him.

"You're one to talk. You were complaining about your legs five minutes ago."

"I wasn't complaining. I was making an observation."

"An observation?"

"That my legs were about to stop working."

Tomas shook his head.

Eric couldn't help smiling.

They weren't in the same training group, so their conversations were usually brief. Still, seeing familiar faces among the recruits made the days feel a little less unfamiliar.

The instructor called for the next group to assemble.

Derrin straightened.

"Well, back to it."

"Try not to lose those legs," Eric said.

"I'll do my best."

Derrin gave him a cheerful grin before heading off with Tomas.

Eric watched them leave for a moment, then returned to his own group.

There was still plenty of work ahead.

By midday, the recruits were more than ready for a meal.

The mess hall was already crowded when Eric arrived. Benches scraped against the floor, bowls clattered, and conversations blended into a steady rumble beneath the wooden rafters.

The smell of warm porridge and cooked vegetables filled the room.

Eric collected his meal and looked around.

He spotted Derrin and Tomas at one of the tables. A couple of other recruits were sitting with them.

"Over here!" Derrin called, raising a hand.

Eric made his way over and placed his bowl on the table.

"Thought you were going to miss lunch," Derrin said.

"I had to wait for my group to finish."

"Well, you're just in time. Tomas was about to eat your share."

"I was not," Tomas replied.

"You were looking at it."

"I was looking at the table."

"Your eyes were definitely on his food."

Tomas gave him a flat look.

Eric sat down, quietly amused.

The meal wasn't anything special. A bowl of porridge, some bread, and a small portion of dried meat. It was simple, but after a morning of training, Eric wasn't about to complain.

For a while, they talked about ordinary things.

Someone at the table complained about the morning exercises. Another recruit mentioned that his arms were still sore from the previous day's training. Derrin had a few comments of his own, though most of them were accompanied by a grin.

"That instructor must have a personal grudge against our legs," Derrin said, tearing off a piece of bread.

Tomas continued eating.

"Maybe your legs just aren't strong enough."

"That's a terrible thing to say to a man who's suffering."

"You're eating perfectly well."

"That's because I'm recovering."

Eric chuckled.

For a little while, the conversation continued.

Then Derrin stopped talking.

It was a small change. His spoon had paused halfway to his mouth, and the smile that usually lingered on his face had disappeared.

Eric noticed.

"What's wrong?"

Derrin didn't answer.

His eyes were fixed on a table a short distance away.

Eric followed his gaze.

Two recruits were sitting there, both looking noticeably smaller than most of the others. They had their meals in front of them, but two larger recruits had stopped beside their bench.

One of them reached down and picked up a piece of bread from the nearer recruit's bowl.

"You won't be needing all of that, will you?"

The smaller recruit looked up.

He didn't say anything at first.

The second bully laughed and took a piece of dried meat from the other recruit's meal.

"What's the matter? You two aren't hungry?"

The two weaker recruits exchanged a glance.

One of them tried to pull his bowl closer.

"P-please don't..." he said quietly. "This is all we have."

The first bully looked down at him.

For a moment, he seemed almost amused.

Then his expression hardened.

"What was that?"

The smaller recruit lowered his eyes.

"I... I said this is all we have."

The bully smacked his hand away from the bowl.

"Don't get smart with me."

The recruit flinched.

"You're small. You don't need to eat that much anyway."

His companion laughed.

"He's right. You'll be fine with what's left."

The bully reached for another piece.

This time, the second recruit tried to stop him, but the bully shoved his hand aside.

"Keep your hands to yourself."

The surrounding tables had grown quieter.

Some recruits glanced over. Others quickly returned to their meals, pretending not to have noticed.

Eric looked back at his own table.

Derrin was still staring.

The cheerful expression he had worn only moments earlier was gone. His spoon rested untouched beside his bowl.

Tomas noticed it too.

"Derrin," he said quietly.

Derrin didn't look at him.

"Don't. Just ignore it."

Derrin's eyes remained on the other table.

"Stop that. Nothing good will come out of it. We're new here. We have enough trouble looking after ourselves."

Eric glanced between them.

He understood Tomas's concern. They were recruits, not soldiers. Getting involved in a confrontation with other recruits could easily bring trouble upon them.

But the sight at the other table was difficult to ignore.

Derrin finally turned his head.

He looked at Eric.

"Do you think so too, Eric?"

Eric blinked.

"Well..."

He looked towards the two smaller recruits, then at the bullies.

He didn't approve of what they were doing. That much was clear.

But what could they actually do?

There were two of them, and the bullies looked considerably bigger. An argument could turn into a fight. And if it did, they might all end up in trouble.

Eric hesitated.

Derrin watched him for another moment.

Then he pushed his bench back.

The legs scraped loudly against the floor.

Tomas closed his eyes briefly.

"There he goes again..."

Derrin stood.

And walked towards the other table.

The two bullies barely noticed him at first.

One was still holding a piece of bread, while the other had taken another piece of dried meat.

Derrin stopped beside them.

"Hey."

The first bully glanced over.

"What?"

"Stop that and give their food back."

The bully stared at him.

Then he scoffed.

"What? You here to donate your food too?"

His companion laughed.

"Don't be stupid. Get lost."

Derrin didn't move.

He looked at the food in their hands, then back at them.

"Seriously. Just give it back."

The first bully's expression became more irritated.

"Didn't you hear me? Get lost."

Derrin looked up at him.

Then, to the bullies' surprise, a small smirk appeared on his face.

"You should stop now."

The bully frowned.

"Or what?"

"Or I'll solve this Derrin's way."

For a moment, neither bully said anything.

Then they burst into laughter.

"Derrin's way?"

"What kind of stupid name is that?"

The first bully stepped closer, looming over him.

"Listen, little man. I don't know who you think you are, but you should learn when to keep your nose out of other people's business."

Derrin tilted his head slightly.

"I was trying to be nice."

"Then try being quiet."

The bully reached out and shoved him.

Derrin took a step back.

His eyes flicked between the two recruits.

One was already moving around the table, while the other looked ready to grab him.

Derrin knew perfectly well that he wasn't going to win a fight against both of them.

He also knew that standing still and letting them hit him wouldn't accomplish anything.

So he did something else.

His hand shot forward.

Before the first bully could react, Derrin snatched the wooden food tray from his hands and darted sideways.

"Hey! What are you—"

Derrin was already moving.

He stepped onto the bench, planted a foot against the edge of the table, and hauled himself up.

The tray was raised above his head.

"Come on!"

The two bullies stared at him.

Derrin grinned.

"Catch me if you can!"

For one glorious second, the mess hall went completely silent.

Then everything happened at once.

"YOU LITTLE—!"

The first bully charged towards the table.

Derrin quickly hopped over a bowl and moved further along the tabletop.

The second bully rushed around the opposite side, trying to cut him off.

Derrin looked down at him.

"You're a bit slow, aren't you?"

"GET DOWN!"

"Why? I'm having lunch."

A nearby recruit hurriedly pulled his bowl out of the way as Derrin stepped past it.

Someone's cup tipped over.

A wooden spoon hit the floor.

The first bully reached for Derrin's ankle, but Derrin skipped away just in time.

"Almost!"

"GET BACK HERE!"

"You're going to have to try harder than that!"

Eric stared.

He had expected an argument.

Perhaps a shove or two.

He had not expected this.

Beside him, Tomas had one hand pressed against his forehead.

"Unbelievable..."

Eric looked at him.

"Is he always like this?"

Tomas didn't answer immediately.

He watched Derrin dart across the table, still carrying the tray while two increasingly furious recruits chased after him.

"...Unfortunately."

At another table, someone began laughing.

That only made the first bully angrier.

He tried to climb onto the bench, but Derrin had already moved away.

"Come on! You're not giving up already, are you?"

"YOU!"

Derrin laughed and hopped over another bowl.

A moment later, the mess hall doors swung open.

"WHAT IN THE NAME OF THE EMPEROR IS GOING ON HERE?!"

A soldier stood at the entrance.

His voice cut through the commotion.

Everyone froze.

The two bullies stopped where they were.

Derrin, however, was still standing on the table.

He slowly looked towards the entrance.

The soldier stared at him.

Derrin stared back.

The tray was still in his hands.

"...I can explain."

"GET DOWN!"

Derrin immediately climbed off the table.

The soldier marched towards them, his expression growing darker with every step.

He looked at the scattered food, the overturned cup, the two angry recruits, and finally at Derrin.

"What happened here?"

Nobody answered at first.

The soldier's eyes narrowed.

"I asked a question."

One of the smaller recruits finally spoke.

"They... they took our food."

The soldier turned towards him.

"What?"

The recruit swallowed.

"They took our bread and meat. We asked them to stop, but they wouldn't."

The soldier looked at the two bullies.

"Is that true?"

One of them opened his mouth.

"Well, they had more than enough, and—"

"I didn't ask for your excuse."

The soldier's voice grew sharper.

"Taking another recruit's food? Intimidating them? And then starting a fight in the mess hall?"

He pointed towards the scattered food.

"Look at this place!"

The first bully lowered his head.

The soldier then turned towards Derrin.

"And you. What exactly were you doing on top of the table?"

Derrin straightened.

"Trying to get their food back, sir."

"By climbing onto the table?"

Derrin glanced at the tray in his hands.

"...It seemed like a good idea at the time, sir."

A few recruits nearby struggled to hide their laughter.

The soldier did not look amused.

"Put that down."

Derrin carefully placed the tray on the nearest table.

"All three of you. Outside. Now."

The reprimand was not short.

The soldier made it very clear that the mess hall was not a place for intimidation, stealing, or settling disputes.

The two bullies were reprimanded for taking another recruit's food and using their size to threaten weaker recruits.

Derrin was reprimanded for creating a disturbance, climbing onto the tables, and turning the mess hall into what the soldier described as "a complete circus."

All three were assigned additional duties.

The two bullies would have to help with cleaning and carrying supplies after training.

Derrin received the same.

He listened without arguing.

The soldier finished by warning them that any further incidents would be reported to the training sergeant.

"Understood?"

"Yes, sir," all three replied.

"Good. Now get out of my sight."

The two bullies exchanged an irritated glance.

The first muttered something under his breath before turning away. His companion followed, and the two returned to their table. Neither looked particularly pleased, but they seemed to have lost their appetite for causing trouble.

Eric watched them go.

Then he noticed the two smaller recruits looking towards Derrin.

One of them hesitated before giving him a small, grateful nod. The other followed shortly after.

Derrin noticed.

He turned towards them, flashed a grin, and gave them a rather smug, triumphant smirk.

It was almost as if he were saying, See? Nothing to worry about.

The two recruits managed faint smiles.

Eric stared.

...Did he really just do that?

He looked at Derrin, then at the two recruits, and finally at the mess hall table that had nearly become a battlefield.

He wasn't quite sure what to say.

Derrin, meanwhile, seemed perfectly satisfied with himself. He turned around and casually made his way back to their table, as though he hadn't just caused half the mess hall to lose their lunch.

What an unbelievable guy.

Tomas was waiting for him.

He looked at Derrin, then at the empty space beside the table where Derrin's meal had been.

"Well?"

Derrin pulled out the bench and sat down.

"What?"

Tomas stared at him.

"That was your plan?"

Derrin picked up his spoon.

"Pretty much."

"You climbed onto a table."

"Yes."

"You stole their tray."

"Borrowed."

"You nearly knocked over half the mess hall."

"Nearly."

Tomas closed his eyes.

"You're impossible."

Derrin grinned and began eating again.

Eric looked at him for a moment.

Then he glanced towards the other table.

The two smaller recruits were eating quietly now. Their food had been returned, and the bullies were nowhere near them.

"You're getting extra duties too," Eric said.

Derrin shrugged.

"Could be worse."

"How?"

"They could've made me clean the whole mess hall."

Tomas gave him a tired look.

"Don't give them ideas."

Derrin laughed.

Eric couldn't help joining in.

The tension that had settled over the mess hall seemed to have faded. Conversations slowly resumed, bowls and spoons clattered again, and the ordinary noise of lunchtime returned.

Derrin carried on eating as though nothing particularly unusual had happened.

Eric watched him for a little longer.

Do you think so too, Eric?

The question returned to his mind.

He hadn't answered.

He still wasn't entirely sure what he would have done in Derrin's position. He understood Tomas's concern. Getting involved could bring trouble, and Derrin had certainly managed to find plenty of it.

But he also couldn't deny what he had seen.

Everyone else had noticed the bullying. Most had simply looked away.

Derrin hadn't.

And when he realized he couldn't win by fighting, he had found another way.

It was ridiculous.

Completely ridiculous.

Eric glanced at the empty tray still sitting on the nearby table.

Derrin's way, huh?

A small smile crept onto his face.

Perhaps there was more than one way to stand up for someone.

"Hey, Eric."

He looked up.

Derrin was holding out a piece of bread.

"You're staring at nothing. Want some?"

Eric looked at the bread, then at him.

"Is this stolen?"

Derrin laughed.

"Of course not!"

Tomas sighed.

"Don't encourage him."

Their laughter blended into the noise of the mess hall.

For the rest of lunch, Derrin seemed perfectly content.

He had earned himself extra duties, nearly caused a small disaster, and probably made himself memorable to half the recruits in the building.

Yet he didn't seem to regret any of it.

The rest of the day passed without anything particularly unusual.

Training continued in the afternoon. Eric spent most of it working through conditioning exercises and basic marching drills. His legs were still sore, and the additional duties assigned to Derrin and the two bullies meant they had to help clean up and carry supplies after the day's training.

Eric caught sight of Derrin later that afternoon.

Even from a distance, he could recognize that familiar grin.

Apparently, the extra work hadn't dampened his spirits.

By the time evening arrived, the recruits were exhausted. They returned to their barracks, washed up, and settled in for the night.

Eric sat on the edge of his bunk, rubbing his tired legs.

The barracks were gradually becoming more familiar. The wooden walls, the rows of bunks, the smell of old blankets and leather equipment. Even the sounds of recruits talking and moving around had started to feel ordinary.

Some were chatting, others were checking their equipment, and a few had already climbed into their bunks.

Eric was just beginning to relax when—

BANG!

The barracks door flew open.

A recruit came rushing inside.

"URGENT! URGENT!"

Several heads turned.

"What?"

"What's going on?"

"Why are you shouting?"

The recruit stumbled to a stop, bent forward with his hands on his knees.

"Huff... huff..."

He tried to speak, but he was still catching his breath.

"Wait... hold on..."

Everyone stared at him.

"Just spit it out!"

"What happened?"

"Did something happen outside?"

The recruit finally straightened, still breathing heavily.

"I... I saw..."

He paused, trying to gather enough breath.

"I saw a notice! They posted it on the notice board near the barracks entrance!"

A few recruits exchanged glances.

"What notice?"

"What does it say?"

"Did something happen?"

The recruit looked around at their faces.

Then he grinned, barely able to contain his excitement.

"Tomorrow! They're giving us a chance to undergo a magic aptitude test!"

For a moment, the room went completely quiet.

Then—

"WHAT?!"

"Magic aptitude test?"

"Wait, are you serious?!"

"Tomorrow?!"

Several recruits immediately got up from their beds.

"Where did you see it?"

"Does it say everyone can take it?"

"Are they really testing us?"

The recruit nodded eagerly.

"It's posted near the barracks entrance! It says all recruits will be given an opportunity to undergo aptitude testing tomorrow!"

The barracks erupted.

Some recruits were practically shouting over one another. Others were grinning, asking questions, or already imagining what it might mean for them.

"Does that mean we might become mages?"

"Imagine that!"

"Do you think they'll teach us magic if we have the aptitude?"

"Who knows! But we finally get to find out!"

Even Tomas looked more attentive than usual.

Derrin leaned forward, his eyes bright.

"Wait, wait. Does that mean we might actually have magic?"

"That's what the notice says!"

"That's amazing!"

The excitement spread through the room.

For most of the recruits, magic was something they had only heard about. Something used by trained mages and knights. Something that seemed to belong to people born with the right talent, or those fortunate enough to receive the opportunity to learn.

Commoners rarely got such a chance.

Most would never even know whether they had the aptitude for magic, let alone receive proper training.

But tomorrow...

Tomorrow, they might finally find out.

For some of them, this could change the course of their entire lives.

Eric remained seated.

He looked around at the excited recruits. Their expressions were filled with anticipation, disbelief, and hope.

He understood that this was a big deal.

But his thoughts were moving in a slightly different direction.

Magic aptitude...

He had almost forgotten about it.

Then another thought struck him.

Wait.

They're testing us for magic?

What the f...

Magic?!
