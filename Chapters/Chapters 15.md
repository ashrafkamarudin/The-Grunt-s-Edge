I was lying down on my bed, halfway through pulling the blanket over me when the tent flap shifts and Mila steps in from the entrance, moving like she’s already decided the day is over. She looks over and finds me still awake.

“You’re still up,” she says.

“Yeah,” I answer.

A small nod. Like that settles it.

“Good,” she says. “No fainting this time.”

I stare at her for a moment. “…That’s still a thing?”

“It’s a useful thing,” she says with a small smile, already dropping down near her usual spot.

Ren shifts once in his sleep but doesn’t wake. Darrin doesn’t move at all. Sutter is still sitting near the support pole, not asleep yet, just quiet in that way he gets when the day is done but the body hasn’t caught up. Pell is turned on his side, eyes closed, but not fully gone either. The tent feels heavier now, like the day is finally letting go of people one by one.

Mila stretches her legs out.

“You did good for a newbie,” she says after a moment, like it’s not something she planned to say out loud.

I glance at her.

She shrugs slightly, eyes still half on nothing. “I was still panicking a lot when it was my second battle.”

That sits there for a second longer than her usual jokes. Then she adds, lighter again, like she’s brushing it off before it can turn into anything.

“So yeah. You’re doing fine.”

"Thanks..." I answer.

I look at her for a moment, then close my eyes. I don’t fall asleep right away. My body is tired enough that it should. But it doesn’t. I just lie there and the fight starts coming back on its own, not the whole thing, just pieces sticking to each other. I review the battles I fought and what I did right and what I did wrong. I lie there for a long time. Not thinking in words anymore, just letting the fragments sit where they landed. The shield wall. The moment my knee hit the ground. Darrin stepping in front of me like it was nothing, like covering someone else’s failure was just part of the rhythm he already knew. The way the spear felt wrong in my hand when I tried to hold two things at once, like my body hadn’t learned yet what my head already understood.

The training was supposed to prepare me for this. And maybe it did, in the ways that matter for most people. But I still froze. Not completely, not enough to die, but enough that someone else had to move for me. And that sits somewhere I can’t shake loose. I open my eyes. The tent is dark now, the last of the evening light gone. I can hear Ren breathing slow and heavy. Someone else shifting in their sleep. The camp outside has gone quiet too, not silent, just muted, like everything is too tired to make real noise anymore. I sit up. Slow. Careful not to wake anyone. My body protests in ways that feel distant, like the soreness belongs to someone else and I’m just borrowing it for now. I pull my boots back on and stand. No one stirs and I step out.

The air hits me first, cooler than I expected, carrying smoke and dirt and the faint edge of something metallic that I’m starting to recognize as just how this place smells after a fight. The camp is still alive in the way a dying fire is alive — embers holding shape without burning anymore. A few figures move between tents, slow and shapeless in the low light. Someone coughs somewhere to my left. No one calls out to me.

I start walking without deciding where. Past the tent rows. Past the mess hall, dark now, the benches empty. Past a cluster of supply crates where two men sit with their backs against the wood, not talking, just existing in the same space like company is enough. I don’t look at them long enough to recognize who they are. My feet carry me past the edge of the camp proper, past the last tent, past the dying watchfires, until the ground opens up beneath my next step — and I stop. Not because I decided to. Because something in my chest already knows where I am before my mind catches up.

The training field.

It looks different at night. Smaller, somehow. During the day it felt vast, every morning stretching out in front of us like something we had to earn our way across. Now it’s just a patch of dirt under a dark sky. The practice dummies are still standing at the far end, their shapes barely visible, arms raised in poses that meant something during drills but look like silent questions now. The equipment racks are half-empty, spears and shields left out because someone decided carrying them back wasn’t worth the extra steps.

I walk to the edge of the field and stop. Then I sit down. Right there on the packed earth, not bothering to find a better spot. I pull my knees up loosely and let my arms rest over them.

There’s nothing to look at. Just the field. The dark. The shape of the dummies. The same ground I stood on during drills, learning how to brace, how to thrust, how to hold formation. It all made sense there. Under the sun, with the instructor’s voice telling me where to put my feet, it felt like I was learning something real.

But the real thing was different. The real thing didn’t care that I knew where to put my feet.

I sit there and stare at the open space until my eyes stop trying to focus on anything. The thoughts don’t stop completely, but they fade, losing edges, becoming background noise that I don’t have to answer to anymore. I’m tired. Not the kind of tired that sleep fixes right away. The kind that just sits in your bones and waits.

The air is still.

The field is empty.

I’m still looking at it when my eyes close. Not because I chose to. Because there’s nothing left to hold them open for.

And just like that, two months pass. The days just… keeps going. Wake up. Drill. Eat. Wound tending in the medical tent. Drill again. Eat again. Sleep . Repeat until the days stop feeling like separate things. There are more fights in between, but none like the Troll. Nothing that breaks the shape of the world the same way. Just smaller pushes, smaller impacts, things that leave bruises instead of rewriting everything.

Larris doesn’t come back .. and Wells did for a while. Not properly.

His leg doesn’t heal right. He tries once, maybe twice, to stand with us during training, but it doesn’t last. After that he’s moved off the line. Discharged. Returned somewhere behind the walls. I don’t know if that’s better or worse. We don’t talk about it much. We just keep going.

My days settle into a rhythm I don’t really choose. Morning drill with spears. Corrections shouted at us until the movements stop being thought and start being reaction. Lunch that tastes the same no matter what day it is. Then the medical tent.

I start helping there for awhile til eventually I find out the healer's name. It's Corrin. What I did there is not real healing. Not like Corrin does it. Just wound tending. Cleaning cuts. Holding cloth down when someone presses too hard. Passing things when asked. Learning what gets infected and what doesn’t. Learning how fast people stop complaining when pain becomes normal instead of new and also learn about herbs and mixing simple medicines. I get better at it slowly.

Then today happens. Something different. The camp feels normal at first, but it isn’t. People are moving with a different kind of attention today, not rushed, just aware, like everyone heard the same thing and is quietly adjusting to it. I catch pieces of conversation as I walk past tents and supply racks, fragments that start disconnected but slowly line up into something clear. “Tester’s here.” “Magic screening today.” “They’re starting with levies first.” A man near the supply crates confirms it when I pass, speaking like it’s already settled history. An arcanist arrived this morning, he says, and they’ll begin with the lower ranks before moving down the line. The word arcanist sticks with me longer than the rest. I don’t stop walking. I just move faster. I need to find the others.

They say someone is coming. A tester. For levies, for regular soldiers, for anyone still standing in the line. Someone who checks if you have aptitude for magic.

Magic.

I sit up a little without thinking. I’ve been wondering about it for a while. Not in a serious way, just… there’s no way to tell on your own. You either have it or you don’t, and you just never know which one it is until someone else looks at you. This is that moment. A real answer, finally. I just move faster and find the others.

I find them in the mess hall, sitting at a table in the corner. They’re eating, but they’re not really eating. They’re talking, but it’s not normal conversation. It’s quiet, focused, like they’re trying to figure something out together. I join them without thinking.

“Did you guys hear? The Arcanist is coming.” I ask.
