Mage Development and Spellforms
Overview
Mages use mana by learning and manipulating structured magical patterns called spellforms. A spellform gives mana a defined structure and directs it to produce a particular effect.

Spellforms are learned from instructors, books and grimoires. A mage does not need to invent a spellform independently in order to learn it.

Mage development has two related but separate measures:
- Mage rank and proficiency: what the individual is capable of doing with mana and spellforms.
- Spellform tier: the complexity and requirements of an individual spellform.
A higher spellform tier does not automatically mean greater destructive power. Complexity, mana invested, output, control and the spell's purpose all matter.
Learning a spellform
Mages learn the shape and structure of spellforms through instruction or written material.

A typical learning process is:
1. Study: Learn the spellform's shape, structure and activation method from an instructor, book or grimoire.
2. Reconstruct: Use mana and mental concentration to reproduce the pattern within the mental world.
3. Imprint, if eligible: Establish the spellform in the mental world so it can be accessed quickly later.
4. Invoke or construct: Supply compatible mana and activate an imprint, or actively construct the spellform when casting without an imprint.
Understanding a spellform and being able to use it are different things. A student may understand a pattern but lack the control or capacity to imprint or cast it.
Imprinted and non-imprinted casting
Imprinted spellforms
Spellforms of Tier 1 to Tier 3 can be imprinted in a mage's mental world.

An imprint is a prepared pattern that the mage can access quickly. The mage supplies mana and activates the pattern rather than reconstructing the entire spellform each time.

Important rules:
- Imprinting reduces preparation time; it does not make a spell free.
- Every activation still consumes mana.
- Imprints remain available after casting unless deliberately removed, replaced or no longer maintained.
- The number of spellforms a mage can maintain varies by individual and by the complexity of the imprints.
- Most mages can maintain only around two or three imprints at a time.
A more complex imprint occupies more mental capacity. Two mages may therefore have different combinations of imprints.
Casting without an imprint
A full Mage can channel a spellform without having it imprinted.

They must remember or consult the spellform's shape and activation method, then actively construct and channel it with mana. This takes more preparation and concentration than invoking an imprint.

For complex spellforms, mages may open a spellbook or grimoire and refer to its diagrams while casting. A book provides the pattern; it does not cast the spell for them.

This gives a Mage a potentially very broad repertoire: they are not limited to the two or three spellforms they have imprinted. Their practical limits are their knowledge, memory, affinity compatibility, ability to construct the pattern, available mana and the time and concentration required.
Spellform tiers
Spellform tiers describe the complexity and mana requirements of a pattern, not simply its destructive power.
Tier	General category	Illustrative examples
Tier 1	Basic utility	Light, simple ignition, small flame
Tier 2	Weak attack, defence or utility	Minor projectile, weak barrier, simple wind push
Tier 3	Standard attack, defence or utility	Fire arrow, air shield, stronger detection or reinforced barrier
Tier 4	Advanced spellforms	More complex, demanding or larger-scale attacks and defences; must be actively constructed and cast
Tier 5	Unsettled	A possible category for exceptionally complex spellforms; see the design note below


Only Tier 1 to Tier 3 spellforms can be imprinted. Tier 4 and higher spellforms must be constructed and cast rather than stored as permanent imprints.

Spellform tier is not a direct measure of damage. A complex defensive or utility spell can be high-tier without being an attack.
Mage rank and proficiency
Mage Apprentice
A Mage Apprentice:
- Has formed a mana core and can manipulate mana.
- Can learn spellforms and imprint eligible patterns (Tier 1 to Tier 3) in their mental world.
- Cannot yet reliably channel a spellform that is not imprinted.
- Relies on their imprints to invoke spells.
An Apprentice may understand a spellform from a book or instructor but still be unable to cast it without first imprinting it.
Mage
A person becomes a Mage when they can channel spellforms without relying on an imprint.

A Mage can:
- Invoke imprinted spellforms quickly.
- Actively construct and channel known, non-imprinted spellforms.
- Use spellbooks or grimoires as references for complex patterns.
- Build a much wider practical repertoire than their limited imprint capacity.
This does not mean literally unlimited casting. A Mage remains limited by mana reserves, control, knowledge, affinity compatibility, memory, concentration and casting time.
Mage proficiency levels
Level	General capability
Basic Mage	Can reliably cast Tier 1 and Tier 2 spellforms.
Intermediate Mage	Can cast up to Tier 3 and can perform Tier 4 spellforms.
Advanced Mage	Can cast Tier 4 spellforms more capably, handle multiple Tier 4 spellforms or potentially cast Tier 5 spellforms.


These are broad capability thresholds, not guarantees that every mage at a given level can cast every spell in that tier. Individual talent, mana capacity, output, control, efficiency and experience matter.
Affinity requirements
Some spellforms have mana-affinity requirements.

Examples:
- A fire-oriented spellform requires compatible fire-affinity mana.
- A wind-oriented spellform requires compatible wind-affinity mana.
- Some simple or general-purpose spellforms may use neutral mana.
A person can study a spellform even if they lack its required affinity, but they cannot successfully imprint or activate an affinity-dependent spellform without compatible mana.

Affinity determines compatibility, not overall strength. A strong fire affinity does not automatically make someone a stronger mage in every respect.

Multiple affinities are possible but uncommon.
What determines a mage's strength?
Mage rank and spellform tier do not tell the whole story. A mage's actual capability depends on factors such as:
- Mana core capacity.
- Mana output.
- Mana control and precision.
- Mana efficiency.
- Mental capacity and stability.
- Knowledge of spellforms.
- Affinity compatibility.
- Experience and combat application.
Two mages using the same spellform can produce very different results because of differences in output, control, efficiency and execution.
Casting constraints
- An imprinted spellform is faster to access, but still consumes mana on activation.
- A non-imprinted spellform requires active construction and generally takes more time and concentration.
- Complex casting can be interrupted.
- Larger or more demanding effects require more mana and control.
- Knowing a spellform does not guarantee the mage has enough capacity or skill to cast it successfully.
- Mages cannot ignore affinity requirements simply by memorizing a pattern.
Tier 5 design note (not yet settled)
Tier 5 has not been finalized.

One possible direction is to define Tier 5 by structural complexity and scale, rather than simply making it a larger attack:
- Multiple interconnected spellform structures.
- Several simultaneous effects or sustained effects.
- Large areas or substantial environmental influence.
- More demanding mana control and longer construction.
- Potentially requiring exceptional concentration or coordinated assistance.
For example, a Meteor Shower should not simply be a larger Fire Arrow. It could require a complex, interconnected spellform that shapes and sustains multiple impacts over an area.

This is a proposal for future development, not established canon. Tier 5 should remain rare and difficult enough that even an Advanced Mage cannot casually cast it.
