Spellforms
Overview
A spellform is a structured magical pattern that directs mana to produce a particular effect. Spellforms are the Empire's standardized method of teaching and practicing magic.

A spellform exists as a pattern that a mage can understand, reconstruct and manipulate within their mental world. It provides mana with structure and instructions for the intended magical effect.

Spellforms are learned from instructors, books and grimoires. Mages do not need to invent a spellform themselves in order to learn or use an established one.
Learning a spellform
A mage generally learns a spellform through the following process:
1. Study the pattern: Learn its shape, structure and activation method from an instructor, book or grimoire.
2. Understand its construction: Study how the pattern is arranged and how mana must flow through it.
3. Reconstruct it: Use mana and concentration to reproduce the pattern within the mental world or during active casting.
4. Imprint it, if eligible: Tier 1 to Tier 3 spellforms can be imprinted into the mage's mental world for quicker access.
5. Activate it: Supply compatible mana and execute the spellform.
Learning the shape does not automatically mean a mage can successfully imprint or cast it. The mage still needs the necessary mana control, mental capacity, affinity compatibility and proficiency.
Imprinted spellforms
Spellforms of Tier 1 to Tier 3 can be imprinted in a mage's mental world.

An imprint is a prepared pattern that the mage can access quickly. When activating it, the mage supplies mana to the established pattern instead of reconstructing the entire spellform.

Key rules:
- Imprinting reduces preparation time; it does not make a spell free.
- Every activation consumes mana.
- An imprint generally remains available after casting.
- A mage can deliberately remove or replace an imprint.
- Imprint capacity varies between individuals.
- Most mages can maintain around two or three imprints at once.
- More complex spellforms occupy more mental capacity.
The exact number and combination depend on the individual's mental capacity, the complexity of each pattern and their ability to maintain stable imprints.
Casting without an imprint
A full Mage can channel a spellform without having it imprinted.

They must remember the spellform's shape and activation method, then actively construct and channel it using mana. This generally requires more preparation, concentration and time than invoking an imprint.

For complex spellforms, a mage may consult a book or grimoire while casting. The book provides the spellform's pattern as a reference; the mage must still construct and channel it.

This means a Mage is not restricted to the two or three spellforms they have imprinted. Their practical repertoire can be much wider, limited by:
- Which spellforms they have learned.
- Their ability to remember or reference the pattern.
- Their mana reserves and output.
- Their mana control and precision.
- Their affinity compatibility.
- The time and concentration needed to construct the spellform.
"Unlimited spells" therefore means access to a broad repertoire, not unlimited mana or the ability to cast without constraints.
Spellform tiers
Spellform tiers indicate the complexity and mana requirements of a pattern. They do not directly measure destructive power.
Tier	General category	Illustrative examples
Tier 1	Basic utility	Light, simple ignition, producing a small flame
Tier 2	Weak attack, defence or utility	Minor projectile, weak barrier, simple wind push
Tier 3	Standard attack, defence or utility	Fire arrow, air shield, stronger detection or reinforced barrier
Tier 4	Advanced spellforms	More complex or demanding attacks, barriers and utility effects
Tier 5	Not yet finalized	Reserved for exceptionally complex spellforms, if adopted


Only Tier 1 to Tier 3 spellforms can be imprinted. Tier 4 and higher must be actively constructed and cast.

A spellform's tier is based on its structure, complexity and demands, not solely on how much damage it causes. A sophisticated defensive or utility spell may have a high tier without being destructive.
Tier 4 and Tier 5
Tier 4
Tier 4 spellforms are too complex to be permanently imprinted. They must be constructed during casting.

They generally demand greater mana control, concentration and preparation than lower-tier spellforms.

An Intermediate Mage can cast Tier 4 spellforms, although the difficulty and reliability depend on the individual spell and mage.
Tier 5 (unsettled)
Tier 5 has not yet been defined as established canon.

A possible direction is to reserve it for spellforms with exceptional structural complexity or scale, such as:
- Multiple interconnected spellform structures.
- Several simultaneous or sustained effects.
- Large-area influence.
- Complex mana manipulation over an extended casting sequence.
- Spellforms requiring exceptional concentration, control or coordinated assistance.
A Meteor Shower, for example, could be a Tier 5 spell if it requires a complex pattern to shape and sustain multiple impacts over a wide area. It should not simply be a larger version of a Fire Arrow.

This remains a design proposal. Tier 5 should be rare and difficult enough that even an Advanced Mage cannot cast it casually.
Affinity requirements
Some spellforms require mana with a particular affinity.

Examples:
- Fire-oriented spellforms require compatible fire-affinity mana.
- Wind-oriented spellforms require compatible wind-affinity mana.
- Water-oriented spellforms require compatible water-affinity mana.
- Earth-oriented spellforms require compatible earth-affinity mana.
- Some basic or general-purpose spellforms can use neutral mana.
A person can study a spellform even if they lack its required affinity. However, they cannot successfully imprint or activate an affinity-dependent spellform without compatible mana.

Affinity compatibility applies to both imprinting and casting.

Affinity determines which spellforms a mage can use, not their overall strength. A strong fire affinity does not automatically make someone a stronger mage in every respect.

Multiple affinities are possible but uncommon.
Casting and mana costs
A spellform does not create mana. The mage must supply the mana needed to execute it.

Mana cost and casting difficulty can depend on:
- The complexity of the spellform.
- The amount of mana invested.
- The size, range and duration of the effect.
- The precision required.
- The mage's control and efficiency.
- Whether the spellform is imprinted or actively constructed.
Imprinted casting is usually faster because the pattern is already prepared. It still consumes mana and requires the mage to activate it correctly.

Non-imprinted casting requires active construction. Complex patterns take more time and concentration, and the mage may be interrupted before completing them.
Spellform knowledge and standardization
The Empire can teach standardized spellforms through instructors, military manuals, academies, books and grimoires.

Spellforms may be grouped by access:
- Common spellforms: Widely taught to apprentices and ordinary mages.
- Military spellforms: Taught to mages serving in military organizations.
- Specialized spellforms: Reserved for particular magical disciplines or assignments.
- Restricted spellforms: Closely guarded by the Empire, institutions or particular organizations.
A mage's knowledge of spellforms is distinct from their personal strength. Knowing a complex pattern does not guarantee that they have the capacity, control or mana reserves to cast it.
Design principles
1. Spellforms are structured patterns that direct mana.
2. Mages learn established patterns from instructors or written references.
3. Tier 1 to Tier 3 spellforms can be imprinted for rapid activation.
4. Tier 4 and above must be actively constructed and cast.
5. Imprinting saves preparation time but does not remove mana costs.
6. Most mages can maintain only two or three imprints, though capacity varies.
7. Some spellforms require specific mana affinities.
8. Spellform tier measures complexity and requirements, not simply destructive power.
9. A mage's knowledge, personal proficiency, mana capacity and spellform tier are separate considerations.
10. Tier 5 remains undecided until its role in the wider magic system is established.
