Currency of the Kingdom
Overview
The kingdom uses five denominations:
1. Copper Bit (B) — the base unit, used for small everyday purchases.
2. Silver Mark (M) — used for wages and ordinary trade.
3. Gold Crown (C) — used for substantial purchases and larger transactions.
4. Grand Crown (GC) — a high-value denomination used for major commercial and property transactions.
5. Royal Sovereign (RS) — the highest denomination, reserved for the wealthiest individuals and institutions.
The higher denominations exist so that large fortunes and major transactions do not require impractical quantities of smaller coins. Most ordinary people will rarely, if ever, handle a Grand Crown or Royal Sovereign.
Exchange Rates
Denomination	Equivalent
1 Silver Mark	20 Copper Bits
1 Gold Crown	20 Silver Marks
1 Grand Crown	20 Gold Crowns
1 Royal Sovereign	20 Grand Crowns


Therefore:
- 1 Gold Crown = 400 Copper Bits
- 1 Grand Crown = 400 Silver Marks
- 1 Royal Sovereign = 400 Gold Crowns
- 1 Royal Sovereign = 8,000 Silver Marks
- 1 Royal Sovereign = 160,000 Copper Bits
Typical Use by Social Group
Social group	Currency most commonly handled
Farmers and labourers	Copper Bits; some Silver Marks
Soldiers and craftsmen	Mostly Silver Marks
Shopkeepers and ordinary merchants	Copper Bits and Silver Marks
Wealthy merchants and minor nobles	Gold Crowns
Great nobles and major merchant houses	Grand Crowns
Royal household and kingdom treasury	Royal Sovereigns


These are general tendencies, not strict restrictions. A person can possess or exchange any denomination if they have the means.
What the Denominations Represent
- Copper Bit: Everyday necessities and small purchases, such as basic food.
- Silver Mark: Wages, regular trade, tools, clothing and other ordinary expenses.
- Gold Crown: Expensive goods, quality equipment, horses and substantial purchases.
- Grand Crown: Large estates, major property deals, merchant-house transactions and significant contracts.
- Royal Sovereign: Exceptional wealth, royal finances and transactions involving the greatest nobles or institutions.
Grand Crowns and Royal Sovereigns may be uncommon in everyday circulation. For very large transactions, they can also serve as recognised accounting or settlement units rather than money that ordinary people routinely carry.
Military Pay: Working Proposal
The following figures are a proposed scale for ordinary soldiers and have not yet been finalised as canon.
- Proposed monthly pay: 15 Silver Marks.
- Proposed regular allowance during service: 5 Silver Marks per month.
- Proposed retained portion: 10 Silver Marks per month, paid when the soldier completes their term.
- Food, lodging, standard equipment and basic medical care are provided separately while serving.
The intention is for a month's total pay to represent roughly two to three months of basic food for one person. This makes military service financially worthwhile for a commoner without making an ordinary soldier wealthy.

A possible additional provision is that, if a soldier dies while serving, retained wages and a defined death benefit are paid to their registered next of kin. The amount and exact terms remain undecided.
Price Scale: Working Reference
Everyday prices and the food-cost assumptions are still provisional. They should be settled together with the military pay scale so that the economy remains internally consistent.

A useful target is for a month's full soldier pay to buy approximately two to three months of basic food for one person. Prices should reflect the difference between bare subsistence staples and a purchased prepared meal; these should not be treated as interchangeable costs.
Canon Status
Established:
- The five denominations and their names.
- The exchange rate of 20 between each adjacent denomination.
- 1 Royal Sovereign equals 400 Gold Crowns.
Provisional and subject to further worldbuilding:
- Soldier pay and how much is paid immediately versus retained.
- Death benefits and next-of-kin arrangements.
- Exact food costs and everyday market prices.
- Whether high denominations are primarily physical coins, certified treasury pieces, or accounting units for large transactions.
