Mana Control and Techniques
Purpose
This document records how practitioners learn to guide and manipulate
mana, and how different methods relate to actual control.

The central principle is that teaching methods are tools, not mandatory
components of magic.
1. Teaching Methods Are Tools
Spellforms, chants, scriptures, prayers, gestures, symbols and similar
practices can help a person learn, remember and reproduce mana
manipulation.

They are not necessarily required ingredients of a spell.

A practitioner with sufficient mana control may manipulate mana
directly, perform a sequence of controlled transitions, or develop a new
way of guiding mana without relying on an established teaching aid.
2. Understanding, Method and Control
Keep three concepts distinct:
  Concept                             Meaning
  Understanding                   The practitioner's mental model or
feel for how to approach and guide
mana.

  Method                          The technique or interface used to
guide mana, such as spellforms,
chants or scriptures.
Control                         The practitioner's practical
ability to manipulate mana
accurately and consistently through
the method they have learned.
Understanding is a way for the practitioner to develop a feel for
controlling mana. It is not, by itself, a substitute for practice.

A person may understand what a spell is supposed to do but lack the
control to perform it. Likewise, someone may have excellent control
using one method but struggle when asked to use another.
3. The Joystick and Keyboard Analogy
Learning a mana-control method is like learning to operate an input
device.

Someone who has mastered a joystick does not automatically know how to
operate a keyboard. They may understand the goal, but their habits,
expectations and control skills have developed around a different
interface.

Similarly:
- A spellform practitioner learns to guide mana through spellforms.
- A chant practitioner learns to guide mana through chants.
- A scripture-based practitioner learns through their religious
  method.
- A direct manipulator learns to control mana without relying on those
  teaching tools.
Changing methods requires the practitioner to develop a new mental model
and a new practical feel for the unfamiliar method.

Experience with one method may help with understanding the intended
result, but it does not automatically transfer as proficiency.
4. Direct Mana Manipulation
A practitioner with good enough control can manipulate mana directly
without relying on a chant, spellform or scripture.

They may guide mana through a sequence of deliberate transitions or
develop their own approach.

This is not necessarily effortless or universally superior. Direct
manipulation still requires the necessary understanding, precision,
control and practice.

Whether direct manipulation is inherently harder than using teaching
tools, or simply a different kind of training, remains open.
5. Technique Characteristics
A technique's method can influence its performance.

Different techniques for the same effect may trade casting speed against
strength, size, stability, efficiency or other qualities.

Example:

  Fireball technique        Casting time   Result
  Spellform-based example   Faster         Smaller or weaker fireball
Chant-based example       Slower         Larger or stronger fireball

This is only an example of two particular techniques. Do not turn it
into a universal rule about all spellforms or chants.
6. Developing New Techniques
Magic is not restricted to a fixed catalogue of established spells.
Practitioners with sufficient understanding and control can develop new
methods of manipulating mana.

A new technique still needs to be learned and practised. Knowing the
desired outcome does not mean the practitioner can immediately reproduce
it.

Eric may eventually create or discover unusual techniques because he
questions established assumptions and connects mana with physical
processes. His approach should still require experimentation, failures
and gradual improvement.
