Magical Traditions and Beliefs
Purpose
Different civilisations and institutions have developed different ways
of understanding, teaching and manipulating mana.

Their explanations may differ even when they are manipulating the same
underlying force. No single tradition needs to possess the complete
truth.

These traditions are not necessarily separate magic systems with
exclusive access to particular effects.
1. Different Traditions Can Produce the Same Effect
Different methods can produce the same or similar magical effects.

For example:
- A conventional mage may create light using spellforms.
- A church practitioner may produce light using holy scriptures,
  prayers or sacred rituals.
- An older practitioner may use chants to guide mana into a similar
  effect.
Fundamentally, these methods manipulate the same mana. The church's
light magic is not inherently a separate divine energy simply because
the church explains it in religious terms.

The people using a tradition may sincerely believe in its explanation.
That belief shapes their teaching and practice, but does not necessarily
describe the underlying mechanics.

3. Traditions Are Teaching Frameworks
A tradition gives practitioners a way to understand and reproduce mana
manipulation.

For example, a fire instructor might teach a student to feel the heat of
a flame and let their mana follow the nature of fire. A priest might
teach that sacred passages guide holy light. An ancient practitioner
might teach that a chant helps the practitioner direct mana.

These explanations can be useful teaching models even when they are
incomplete or inaccurate descriptions of what mana fundamentally is.
4. Different Methods Can Have Different Results
Two methods can produce the same kind of spell while differing in
casting speed, strength, stability, efficiency or other characteristics.

For example, two traditions may both teach a fireball:
- One spellform technique may be quick to cast but produce a smaller
  or weaker fireball.
- One chant-based technique may take longer but produce a larger or
  stronger fireball.
These are characteristics of those particular techniques, not universal
rules that all spellforms are fast or all chants are powerful. Another
spellform could be powerful, and another chant could be quick.

The method can influence the result, but does not dictate it absolutely.
5. Learning Another Tradition
In principle, a person can learn another tradition. In practice, this is
often difficult and relatively rare.

The main obstacle is frequently the practitioner's established
understanding.

A mage who has spent years believing that spellforms are what move or
guide mana may struggle to accept that chants can achieve the same
thing. Their understanding, expectations and habits are built around one
approach. Learning another requires them to rethink and rewire that
mental model, then practise the unfamiliar method.

Prior experience may help someone understand the intended result, but it
does not automatically give them proficiency with a different method.

Learning multiple traditions is possible, but uncommon.
6. No Tradition Is Automatically Superior
Different traditions have different strengths, limitations and areas of
specialisation. Their practitioners may have accumulated deep practical
knowledge even if their explanations of mana are incomplete.

Do not portray conventional practitioners as foolish simply because they
do not share Eric's perspective. Their methods work, and generations of
experience have refined them.
