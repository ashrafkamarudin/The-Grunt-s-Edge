Mana
Purpose of This Document
This document records the foundational understanding of mana for future
worldbuilding and revisions. It is primarily an author reference, not an
explanation that needs to be given to readers all at once.

Keep two layers separate:
- In-world understanding: What different people, cultures and
  magical traditions believe about mana, and how they have learned to
  manipulate it.
- Underlying reality: How mana actually behaves. This deeper layer
  is retained for consistency and for discoveries Eric may make later.
The reader should encounter the system naturally through the story.
Avoid front-loading technical explanations.
1. Mana Is Universal
Mana exists throughout the world and is present in all things, including
living organisms, physical matter and the surrounding environment.

People can learn to manipulate mana, but different civilisations do not
necessarily understand it in the same way. They have developed different
explanations, philosophies, teaching traditions and practical methods.

There is no requirement that one civilisation has the complete or
universally accepted explanation.
2. Pure Mana and Different Mana States
Pure Mana
Pure mana is the default, neutral state of mana. A Mana Core stores pure
mana.
Mana States
Mana can behave in different ways. The underlying author model takes
inspiration from particle behaviour:
- Fire-oriented mana: Particles vibrate rapidly, representing a
  higher-energy state.
- Cold-oriented mana: Particles move or vibrate more slowly,
  representing a lower-energy state.
- Other states: Other mana behaviours may have their own
  characteristic patterns. These do not all need to be defined now.
These are different states or behaviours of the same underlying mana,
not necessarily separate substances.

The terms fire mana, cold mana and similar elemental labels are
useful descriptions. They are not necessarily the literal truth of what
mana is.
3. Changing Mana States
Changing mana from one state to another requires energy. Pure mana can
be used to drive that transition, and some mana is lost during the
conversion.

The amount retained depends on conversion efficiency.

Illustrative example, not fixed numerical canon:

  Conversion                                  Starting amount   Resulting fire-oriented mana
  High efficiency: pure to fire-oriented                  100                             95
Lower efficiency: pure to fire-oriented                 100                             70

The starting state also matters. Pure mana is neutral, so converting it
into fire-oriented mana can be more efficient than changing mana already
in another state. For example, changing cold-oriented mana into
fire-oriented mana is a more demanding transition and may involve
greater loss.

Keep these concepts distinct:
- Conversion efficiency: How much starting mana becomes the
  desired state.
- Conversion loss: Mana not retained as the desired state during
  the transition.
Exact energy accounting and numerical limits remain undecided.
4. Affinity as a Developed Specialisation
Mages commonly call their developed efficiency with a particular mana
state an affinity. This is the world's terminology and explanation,
not necessarily a literal elemental property a person is born with.

A mage may develop what people call fire, water, wind or earth affinity.
In practical terms, their body has become more efficient at producing or
handling mana with those characteristics.

Affinity develops through exposure, experience and deliberate practice.
For example, a person training towards fire affinity might spend time in
hot environments, practise sensing mana there, and repeatedly practise
converting pure mana into fire-oriented mana.

Exposure helps them become familiar with the relevant characteristics.
Deliberate practice develops their ability to manipulate mana into that
state efficiently.

A person may develop more than one affinity, although each takes time
and practice. The limits and speed of this development remain open.
5. Environmental Mana
Environmental mana varies in density and characteristics. The physical
environment influences the mana around it.

For example:
- Hot or volcanic places may have local mana characteristics that
  support heat- or fire-oriented effects.
- Cold places may have local mana characteristics associated with cold
  or slower movement.
- Other environments may influence mana in other ways.
Environmental characteristics are not personal affinities.

Keep three concepts separate:
  Concept                             Meaning
  Mana state                      The behaviour or state into which
mana has been changed.

  Mage affinity                   A mage's developed efficiency at
producing or handling a particular
mana state.
Environmental mana              Mana already present around the
mage, with its own density and
characteristics.
6. Environmental Support for Spells
A magical effect does not necessarily disappear when the caster stops
actively channeling mana.

Magical fire, for example, can continue burning by drawing on
environmental mana. It disappears when the mana supporting the magical
effect is depleted or no longer sufficiently available, not simply
because the original caster stops channeling.

Environmental conditions influence how well the effect is sustained. A
cold environment may make a fire effect weaker or more difficult to
sustain, while a warm or fire-supportive environment may help it.

This differs from ordinary physical combustion. A real fire can continue
through fuel and oxygen without environmental mana sustaining the
combustion itself. If magic assists that physical fire, the magical
assistance still depends on mana.
7. Eric and the Two-Way Relationship
The conventional magical framework tends to focus on:

Mana → World

People study how mana can be manipulated to influence the world.

Eric also considers the reverse relationship:

World ↔ Mana

He connects mana to physical principles and asks how the physical world,
its materials and processes influence mana. He may then use physical
processes alongside magic rather than assuming mana must perform every
part of a task.

This is a difference in approach and understanding, not automatic
superiority. Eric must still observe, experiment, make mistakes and
learn.
8. Open Details
- The exact underlying nature of mana and the full range of its
  possible states.
- The precise energy accounting and limits of state conversion.
- How quickly affinities develop, how far they can develop and whether
  they can weaken.
- The relationship between environmental mana density and its
  characteristics.
- How much environmental mana a spell can draw upon and how quickly it
  is depleted.
- The full range of effects that environmental mana can support or
  hinder.
